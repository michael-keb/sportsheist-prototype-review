What you're looking at

The room the brand has never owned

Backchat's home — Fan Stadium plus the two Locker Room lists — is a designed frame. The inside of a room is not. In the app source we hold there is no room page at all, and no real-time audio dependency: pubspec.yaml declares fifty packages across lines 31–81 and not one of them is an audio or video transport. The only realtime packages are socket_io_client and web_socket_channel, which carry events, not voice — and the one socket file in the app listens for exactly two of them, both about idea votes. So nothing in this checkout can open a microphone, let alone run a room.

That does not mean nobody built it. The attendees screen is capture-backed: a real device screenshot exists. Our reading of the evidence is that this is a rebrand of a screen that exists in a build newer than the source we were given, not a proposal we invented. Three things point that way. First, the captures and the code disagree about the whole of Backchat, not just the rooms — the room list renders near-white cards on a white scaffold in the code and black cards on a deep aubergine canvas in the shots, and that list's Dart file is one we do hold, so no third-party room SDK can explain it. Second, the captured screens are drawn in the app's own constants: the lilac #AA95F0, the gold title #FFC721 and the Bebas Neue face are all declared in this repository even though the screens that use them are not. Third, the same pattern shows up elsewhere — the War Room captures show a 5 votes quorum, and no such figure exists anywhere in the app source or its English strings. A checkout that lags the screenshots explains all of it; a third-party SDK explains none of it.

The practical consequence is the same either way: we have no code to rebrand against, so every measurement here is taken from the capture and the design kit, and the client should be told that plainly. What the app does already have is the roster's data — the room record returned by GET /back-chat-rooms/{uuid}/ carries a host list with names and avatars, an invited-member list, and both total_speakers and total_members. The screen has a source. It just has no screen.

The design decision that drives the first two frames is the stage. Instead of the grain-textured locker illustration and the blue-violet room glow, the speaker grid sits on the brand's own field — the same 112deg green gradient as every hero — so the room reads as part of SportsHeist the moment it opens. The attendees frame then reuses that same speaker tile on the kit's section container, so a person looks the same on both sides of one tap. In the capture they do not: the two people on the roster render in a different family, case and weight from the room they are standing in.

They are three screens, so they get three devices above — nothing stacks in one scroll. Each screen carries its own bottom chrome rather than sharing a single pinned bar, since one pinned bar cannot belong to three screens at once.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Tokens — surfaces, hairlines, accent, hero gradient, radii and the type scale, copied verbatim from the sampled set
  • Hero field — the 112deg gradient and faint white triangles, reused as the room stage
  • Status pill — the LIVE NOW geometry and type, relabelled Live
  • Section container — the bordered wrapper that holds the listener stack
  • Icon button — 38 px circle on #1C2129 with a 1.9-stroke white glyph
  • Bottom bar construction — the control dock is the tab bar's pill, height, padding and shadow, with different contents
  • Screen title and avatar rules — 34/800 uppercase, portraits cover-cropped at center 22%
  • Photography — the full set of sixteen distinct portraits from the export set, no face used twice
  • Attendees, from the capture — the screen exists as a device screenshot: a pushed roster split into HOSTS and AUDIENCE, avatar-with-name tiles, a mic-state badge on hosts only, a back arrow as the sole exit. Structure, split and content are the capture's; the styling is ours.
  • Roster data — the room record already returns a host list with names and avatars, an invited-member list, total_speakers and total_members

New in this proposal

  • The whole screen — no Locker Room interior exists in this brand; both frames are new
  • The pitch as a stage — replaces the grain-textured locker illustration and the blue-violet room glow, which is the only blue-shifted colour in the product
  • Speaker tile — portrait, ring, state badge, level meter, role tag, name and state word as one component
  • Four-signal speaking state — ring, badge, weight and word, so the state survives colour-blindness and a still screenshot
  • Control dock — mute, hand, react and Leave, with on-state borrowed from the active tab and open-state from the accent wash
  • Leave as a neutral pill — deliberately not the semantic red
  • Reaction tray — six named reactions drawn as inline SVG, replacing emoji
  • Floating reaction discs — the ambient feedback layer over the stage, with a count chip
  • Monogram avatar fallback — token fill and white letter, replacing an API-supplied colour behind white text measured at 2.3:1 and unfixable on the client while the fill is data
  • Pre-join frame — stat line, listener preview, permission card and Join action; the shipped flow drops you straight into the room
  • Two-line title clamp — the shipped title bar has no wrap or truncation rule
  • One tile for a person, everywhere — the roster uses the room's speaker tile rather than a second avatar treatment. In the capture the same two people render in a different family, case and weight one tap apart
  • Counts on the roster — 6 hosts and 214 in the audience. The capture has no count on either heading and none in the header
  • A shell for the roster — the capture puts attendees straight onto bare canvas with two thirds of the screen empty; here each role sits in the kit's section container
  • Denser audience tile — 56 px four-up, because a roster of 214 at the capture's 145 pt is roughly seventy screens of scrolling
  • List note — states that the audience keeps loading; the capture has no loading, empty or overflow treatment for this screen at all

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Can we have the build these screenshots came from?

    This is the first question and it blocks the others. The source we were given cannot render any of these three screens, and it disagrees with the screenshots about the whole of Backchat — including the room list, whose code we do hold. The most likely explanation is simply that our checkout is older than the captures: someone has built the attendees screen, and we are measuring a photograph of it rather than its code. If a newer branch exists, three things change at once — we would be rebranding real screens instead of reconstructing them, we could check our states against real ones, and the "no audio stack" finding below would need re-testing against that branch rather than this one.

  2. Build the attendees screen, or retire the spec?

    Our recommendation is build it, and it is now built. The reasoning is that it is the cheapest screen in the whole set and the only one whose data already exists: the room record returns the host list, the invited-member list and both counts today, so the roster is a view over data the API already sends. It also cost almost nothing to draw — it introduces one new element, a count pill, and otherwise reuses the room's speaker tile and the kit's section container. Retiring it would have left the people button in the live room leading nowhere and left the one capture-backed spec in the set with no answer. The caveat is honest and should be said out loud in the review: this screen cannot ship before the room it belongs to exists, and the room needs an audio stack that this codebase does not have. It is a design answer waiting on a runtime, not a runtime gap.

  3. Does the audience roster need search and an order?

    The capture has neither, and neither is built here. At 214 people that is a real problem: the product's own search placeholder already reads "Search for rooms, hosts or attendees…", so the vocabulary exists even though the control does not. We also do not know what order the list is in — arrival, alphabetical, or whatever the API returns. We have assumed API order, no search because that is what the capture shows. Tell us if either should change; a search field is a small addition, an order is a backend decision.

  4. Can a host act on someone from this list?

    The capture has no tap target on an attendee at all — no long-press, no mute, no remove, no profile. We have matched it: the roster is read-only. That is defensible for a member and hard to defend for a host, who has no other place to moderate a room. If hosts need to invite up, mute or remove from here, the tile needs an affordance and this screen needs a permission model, and both should be designed now rather than bolted on.

  5. Can we ship one colour for "live"?

    Across Backchat and the rooms the live state is currently drawn five different ways — amber with a dot, a violet pill with a pink dot, a dark-violet pill with a white dot, framework red, and one genuinely live room with no marker at all. This build uses one accent pill, everywhere, and gives the room's own pill a slow pulse so live-ness reads without a second hue.

  6. Who is allowed to speak, and how do they get there?

    The build assumes any listener can raise a hand and the host decides who comes up, and it says so on screen. It also assumes you are always muted on entry and that the OS microphone prompt is deferred until the host actually invites you. If moderation works differently — co-hosts, pre-approved speakers, an open floor — the stage and the dock both change.

  7. Is the six-reaction set right, and is it fixed?

    Clap, Fire, Goal, Love, Wow, Boo are drawn as inline SVG because the contract forbids emoji, and six is what fits the tray at 375 wide. Boo is the one worth a decision — it is the only negative reaction and it is aimed at people in the room. Confirm the set, and confirm whether it is per-club configurable, because a variable set cannot use a fixed-width tray.

  8. What is the ceiling on the speaker grid?

    Three columns holds six speakers comfortably and nine at a push. The pre-join stat line already claims 6 speaking. If a room can promote twenty people the stage needs a scroll or a second tier, and we should design that now rather than discover it in a busy match room.

  9. Do avatar colours come from the API?

    The shipped app fills the fallback avatar disc with a per-user colour supplied by the API and sets the monogram in white, which measures 2.3:1 and cannot be fixed on the client while the fill is data. Here the fill is a token and the letter is white — 18.8:1, and no server value can change it. That means dropping per-user avatar colour entirely, which is a product decision, not a styling one.
