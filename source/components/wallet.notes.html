Where a member sees what they can spend, what is being held, and where every movement went. Four frames under Profile: the balance, the credit history, the filter sheet over it, and the purchase screen. It is also the screen that has to settle what the spendable thing is actually called.

What you're looking at

A balance, a ledger, and a naming problem

Posting an idea costs a deposit. Voting in Support costs a unit. Both come out of a balance the member can top up with real money. That makes this the only screen in the product where money is at stake, so it is built like a bank statement rather than a game screen: every figure is tabular, every amount aligns on its decimal, and every row carries the balance after it so a member can check the arithmetic themselves. The eight rows in frame 2 do reconcile — read the balance column downwards and it runs 940.0 → 965.0 → 966.0 → 941.0 → 966.0 → 466.0 → 470.0 → 420.0.

The balance is deliberately split in two. Available is what can be spent; On deposit is what is currently held against live ideas and will come back. A single total hides the most common question a member has — why can't I post, I have 1,240? — so the total is shown as the headline figure and immediately broken into the two halves beneath it.

The second half of frame 1 does something a wallet screen does not normally do: it explains the rules. Four rows say what a deposit is, when it comes back, the one case where it does not, and what a vote costs. Then a two-cell panel states plainly that Credits and Merits are different things — one is a spendable balance, the other is earned standing — because the live build currently blurs them, and a member who thinks their standing can be bought has misunderstood the entire product.

The four are four separate screens, so each one gets its own device above, numbered and captioned. Nothing on this page is a long scroll of stacked screens, and no review label sits inside a screen.

  • Tokens, radii, type scale — copied from the reference implementation, not approximated
  • Content — the balance split, description / date / signed amount, the three filter buckets, all deposit and refund wording, and the 1 Credit → 3 Merits rate, all taken from the live build and its string table

New in this proposal

  • The whole screen — there is no wallet, balance or history frame anywhere in the Figma export
  • The name "Credits" — one name replacing four; Participation Credits, Participation Deposit as a noun, and BOND are all retired
  • Available / on-deposit split — the core idea, and the reason the screen exists; Locked becomes On deposit
  • Decimal-aligned amount — fixed sign cell, forced single decimal, tabular figures, so a column of amounts reads as a statement
  • Running balance-after — with its bal tag so it cannot be misread as a second amount. The live rows show an amount only
  • Transaction types — five product-facing values where the API has three (deposit, deduct, hold)
  • Direction grammar — arrow plus sign plus type word, never hue alone. The live build carries direction in colour and a bare - / +, and its hold type has no sign at all
  • Date grouping — the live list is a flat scroller with a dd/MM/yy stamp per row
  • "What credits are for" — the four rule rows; the mechanic is currently explained nowhere in the product. The fourth row is the one that says the member can lose the deposit, and it is stated in the same place and the same voice as the three that say they keep it
  • "Credits are not Merits" — the two-cell comparison panel and its one-way-link footer
  • Filter sheet — the date-range group, the two selection modes, Apply as a commit step, Reset, the grab handle, and the two pills that report the filter after the sheet closes. The live sheet has none of these: it filters by type only and commits on each tap
  • Bundle picker — amounts, unit rates, idea equivalents and the best-value tag. The live Purchase credits button has no handler, no translation key and no destination
  • Purchase summary — credits added, resulting balance, total to pay
  • Semantic red tokenised — --red: #E2564A and --red-wash: rgba(226,86,74,.12), the contract's red at the same alpha as the accent wash, used for debits and nothing else

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. What is the spendable thing called?

    This is the screen that cannot be built until you answer it. The live build uses four names for one concept — Credits, Participation Credits, Participation Deposit and a legacy BOND — and the string table still ships an unsubstituted literal, [x] PARTICIPATION CREDITS, which will reach a member's screen exactly as written. We have used Credits as the currency noun and deposit in lower case for the held portion, treating it as something that happens to Credits rather than a second currency. Create idea and Results & Actions have both been brought onto this reading — they previously said Participation Credits and lower-case credits respectively — so the three money screens now use one noun. Confirm it and we will use it in every string in the product. The same pass should fix vi.json, which stores "REPUTATION\nPOINTS" with a hard line break.

  2. Merits are standing, not money. Confirm.

    The comparison panel states it flatly: Credits are a balance you buy, deposit, spend and get back; Merits are a score you earn by taking part, never spend and never buy, and they set your rank. The footer commits to a one-way link — a vote in Support that passes costs 1.0 Credit and earns 3 Merits, and Merits can never be turned back into Credits. If Merits are spendable, or if they can be purchased or gifted, this panel is a lie and both this screen and the Merits tab need rethinking. It is the single most important product statement on the screen.

  3. Settled: a deposit comes back unless the idea lapses.

    This screen used to say a deposit comes back, full stop, while the Results screen showed a Ruled out verdict taking it. Both could not be true. The live string table decides it, and frame 1 now states the rule in full:

    • Returned the moment the idea becomes a Proposal — ideaPage.agreement: "refunded only when the Idea becomes a Proposal"
    • Returned if the idea is cancelled because the match ended, or if a scheduled idea is cancelled — notification.auto_cancel, scheduled_idea.noti_cancel
    • Nothing further is charged once it is a Proposal — proposalPage.free when proposal vetoed: "This will be free when the Proposal is Vetoed, Cancelled or Ruled Out"
    • Forfeited in exactly one case: the idea lapses without enough Likes — notification.idea_lapsed

    So the fourth rule row is the honest version of the promise, and the Results screen now reads deposit already returned on Ruled out and deposit forfeited on Lapsed. Create idea's consent line already agreed with this and is unchanged.

    Still open, and it is a build question rather than a copy one: the ledger has no movement for a forfeiture. A forfeited deposit leaves On deposit and never returns to Available, so the running column silently loses 25.0 with no row to explain it. That needs a sixth type — Forfeit, a debit against the on-deposit half — and a sixth row in the filter sheet, which would move the header pill off 4 of 5 types. We have not invented it here; it is a real gap and we would rather you confirm the shape than guess at it.

  4. Is the running balance the available balance or the total?

    We used available, because that is the number a member can act with and it makes every row a signed movement that reconciles down the column. The cost: a deposit reads as money leaving, when in fact it moves from Available to On deposit and the total is unchanged. If the column should show the total instead, deposits and refunds stop having a direction and the row has to be redesigned.

  5. The API's "deposit" means the opposite of the product's "deposit".

    In credit_history.dart, type: "deposit" renders green with a leading + — credits added. In the product language a participation deposit is credits held. Same word, opposite directions, and hold is a third type with no sign at all. We used the product meaning throughout, so Deposit is a debit. Either the API fields get renamed to credit / debit / hold, or the product word does.

  6. Are the figures and the prices right?

    Everything numeric here is placed to be replaced: a 25.0 deposit per idea, 1.0 Credit per vote in Support, 3 Merits per passing vote, and four bundles running 100 / 500 / 1,200 / 2,500 Credits at $4.99 / $19.99 / $39.99 / $74.99 in USD, priced so the rate improves with volume from $0.050 to $0.030. No pricing of any kind exists in the code, so all of it is invented. The idea-count equivalents on each bundle derive from the 25.0 deposit and move with it.

    The deposit figure is now shared, and it is this screen's. Create idea showed 50 and Results & Actions showed 250, so a client reading any two of the three screens saw two different prices for posting one idea. All three now say 25, and this screen's number won because it is the only one with arithmetic behind it: 300.0 on deposit across 12 live ideas is 25.0 each, and the running-balance column reconciles at 25. That does not make 25 correct — it is still invented — but it makes it consistent, and it means one product decision now moves all three screens instead of three. One thing to note if you change it: this screen writes 25.0 under its always-one-decimal rule while the other two write whole 25. Same figure, two formats. Tell us if one format should win everywhere.

  7. Card on file, or Apple and Google in-app purchase?

    We drew a saved card, because the user model already carries a stripeCustomerId and the back end is on dj-stripe. But Credits are consumable in-app currency, and both app stores require their own purchase flow for that. If the stores' rules apply, frame 4 becomes a native purchase sheet, the prices become store price tiers, and the payment-method row disappears entirely. Nothing else on the screen changes, but that frame does — completely. This is the largest open question here and it is commercial, not visual.

  8. Are Credits fractional?

    Every balance is rendered to one decimal place — 940.0, 1,240.0 — and the amount column is engineered around that decimal so a column stays aligned. We took the decimal from the live build's NumberFormat("#,##0.0"). Nothing in the product ever moves a fraction of a Credit. If Credits are whole numbers, saying so lets us drop the decimal, tighten the 84 px figure column and simplify the amount component.

  9. What happens at zero?

    There is no empty or insufficient-balance state anywhere in the live build. The interesting case is a member whose whole balance is on deposit, who reads 1,240.0 and still cannot post; that needs its own treatment both on the balance card and at the point of posting, probably with a route straight into frame 4. We would also want to know whether a forfeit can ever push a balance below zero before drawing it. Beyond that, the purchase frame says nothing about tax, receipts or refunds — all of which sit upstream of the design.
