What you're looking at

One field, one row, one switch

Settings screens fail by accumulation. Every new preference arrives with its own control, and after a year there are four field heights, three kinds of toggle and two ideas about where a label goes. So this proposal is deliberately narrow: there is one field anatomy, one row height, and one switch, and every screen here is built from those three parts plus copy.

The field is a 64 px box with its label above its value and a single glyph at the trailing edge. That glyph is the whole affordance grammar: a padlock means you cannot change this, a pencil means you can, a chevron means it opens a list. Locked fields also sit on the recessed surface with their value dropped to secondary grey, so a member can tell what is editable by scanning the right-hand edge rather than by tapping things and finding out.

The switch puts the accent on the track and never on the knob, which keeps the on-state reading as a filled channel rather than a green dot — legible at a glance down a list of nine. And each screen is honest about when it commits: the security screen says outright that everything takes effect immediately and there is nothing to save, while the other two carry a sticky bar that counts the changes and stays disabled until there is something to save.

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Screen title — 34/800 uppercase at the gutter
  • Section label — 18/600 white, no adornment
  • Section container — the bordered #080A0C card with its 1 px hairline
  • Status pill — the LIVE NOW treatment, reused for the session count
  • Icon button — 38 px circle, white 1.9-stroke glyph
  • Pill button — the 34 px secondary control
  • Row rhythm — hairline-divided rows with an unbordered first child
  • Avatar — circle, cover fill, center 22% position
  • Semantic red — #E2564A, used only for destructive and downward
  • Tokens, radii, type scale — copied from the reference implementation

New in this proposal

  • The entire dark treatment — these four screens ship today in a light theme; nothing here is a restyle of an existing dark frame, because none exists
  • The single field anatomy — one 64 px box for locked, editable, select, masked and invalid
  • Affordance grammar — padlock, pencil, chevron, read from the trailing edge
  • Locked / You-can-edit group tags — editability declared at the group rather than discovered per field
  • The switch — accent on the track, knob in accent-ink
  • Reserved error space — validation never reflows the screen
  • Strength meter — four segments, five readings, length as a gate rather than a point
  • Device glyph set — seven glyphs including the unknown-device fallback
  • Session row — current-device pill, in-place sign-out, and the red suspicious variant
  • Master / dependent pattern — pause without unsetting, with a live count that keeps its figures
  • Sticky commit bar — with its change count and its blocked-reason line
  • Destructive card — the one red surface, with a consequence sub-line
  • All copy — including the promise that support and account email is always sent and is not a preference

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. The account area is light today. Does all of it move?

    Everything else in this set is a dark screen being redesigned as a dark screen. This one is not: the account sub-pages currently ship as light-theme screens inside a dark app, so a member tapping Account details today falls through a seam. We have re-authored all four in the brand on the assumption that the whole account area goes dark. That is a bigger commitment than a restyle — every field, every switch and every native control in the account tree has to come with it, including anything not captured here. If instead the account area is meant to stay light deliberately, then the seam needs designing rather than removing, and that is a different piece of work.

  2. Which five fields are locked, and which three are the member's?

    We have locked name, username, date of birth, account email and mobile number, and left the three address fields editable — the reading being that anything verified against an identity document cannot be self-edited, and anything that is just a postal address can. Username is the arguable one: it is not on a document, but changing it breaks every attribution in Backchat and the standings. The screen tells a member to ask support to change a locked field, which is only honest if that route exists.

  3. Does changing a password sign out every other device?

    Frame 2 promises it twice — in the intro and again in the commit note the moment the form becomes valid. It is the right behaviour and it is what a member expects, but it is a statement about session handling that we cannot verify, and it is the sort of promise that generates support tickets when it turns out to be untrue.

  4. Is SMS the only second factor, and is 30 days the real retention?

    The two-factor card commits to a one-time pin sent to the member's mobile number, and says turning it on starts the check straight away — so there is no enrolment screen, which is a decision as much as an omission. Separately, the recent-sign-ins list ends with Only the last 30 days are kept. Both are assumptions. If an authenticator app or a passkey is in scope, the card needs a method row rather than a bare switch.

  5. What actually happens when a member deletes their account?

    The destructive row states it plainly: Permanent. Your ideas, votes and merits go with it. That is the strongest and clearest version, but it has consequences the product may not want — removing a member's votes retrospectively changes the counts on settled proposals, and removing their ideas removes things other members backed. The likely real answer is that the account is closed and the contributions are anonymised, in which case this sentence is wrong and needs rewriting before it goes anywhere near a member.

  6. Is the nine-setting notification list the right list?

    Six topics and three channels, with the topic choices preserved when a channel is turned off, and a note that support and account email is always sent and is not a preference. The topics are drawn from what the product currently does, so they will drift as features land. Worth confirming the split is the right one — topics and channels as two independent axes — before the list grows, because the alternative shape is a grid, and a grid at this width does not work.
