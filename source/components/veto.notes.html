Veto is the club's power to stop a member decision it cannot legally or contractually carry out. These four frames cover the moment a member is told their proposal has been frozen, the full rationale behind it, the club-side confirmation that places it, and how a veto sits alongside approved and rejected in the results cards.

What you're looking at

A veto is a hold, not a verdict

This is the most delicate screen in the product. A member has posted a proposal, the club has voted on it, and management is now stopping it. If that reads as rejected, the member concludes the vote was theatre. If it reads as broken, they conclude the app failed. It has to read as held — paused, reversible, and with a reason attached.

The palette gives us one accent, #9BE222, and one semantic red, #E2564A. Neither is right: acid means the club decided yes, red means the club decided no. So the hold is not given a third colour at all. It is given a different kind of mark: the accent, struck through with 45° bars. A filled plate means a decision was reached; bars across the accent mean it was interrupted. The stroke equivalent is a dashed arc — the gauge on a held card is drawn in dashes rather than a solid sweep, so a figure that has stopped moving looks like one.

Two things this replaces. The live build renders veto in amber #DFAA11, which is also its celebration colour — the same hue says "you won" and "you have been overruled". And it renders the frozen countdown in grey, which reads as disabled: the clock looks broken rather than paused. Here the held clock keeps full-contrast white digits on a hatched panel with a pause glyph, and it sits beside a second, plainly-drawn clock — the appeal window — which is the one thing still running. Held is loud. Dead is quiet. The live build has it the wrong way round.

The member-facing copy does three things in order: it says nothing has been decided, it names who holds it and why in their own words, and it shows the way out. The words Veto, Idea to Reject the Veto, Participation Deposit and Support are the live build's own vocabulary and are kept.

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Card shell — 268 px / 16 px padding / --r-card / inset 0 1px 0 hairline, byline row, title, body, meta row and gauge panel, all from idea-card.html.
  • Ring gauge — r27 in a 64 box at 7 px stroke, rotated -90deg, from idea-card.html.
  • Countdown glyph and readout — the stopwatch icon and 13.5/600 tabular time from idea-card.html.
  • Section label, eyebrow, icon button, row rules — 18/600/-.015em, 10.5/700/.14em, --surface circle, 1px --line-soft, all from merits.html.
  • Portraits — four of the eighteen from _assets/avatars.css, inlined as data URIs.
  • Vocabulary and behaviour — VETO HOLD, Idea to Reject the Veto, Participation Deposit, MIN LEFT, the accept / appeal pair, and the deposit-refund rule, from lib/pages/veto/veto_hold.dart, confirm_page.dart and en.json → vetoPage.*.
  • The ø verdict glyph — the vetoed badge on WR-20.
  • Club crest plate — the canonical plate settled in club-selection.html, at the 40 px size, in its code fallback form.

New in this proposal

  • The hold treatment — accent bars at 135deg, rgba(155,226,34,.11), 5 px on / 6 px off, over --sunken with a rgba(155,226,34,.30) ring. Derived entirely from #9BE222; no new hue enters the palette. This replaces amber #DFAA11, which the live build also uses to celebrate.
  • The struck fill — rgba(3,4,1,.62) bars over an acid progress fill, so a held figure is legible as held on a bright plate as well as a dark one.
  • The dashed arc — pathLength="100" with an enumerated dash list giving an exact 75% dashed sweep. The stroke equivalent of the hatch.
  • Held clock ≠ disabled clock — full-contrast white digits on a hatched panel with an accent pause glyph, paired with a plainly-drawn live appeal clock. The live build greys the frozen clock, which reads as broken.
  • The category rule — filled plate = decided, hatched plane = held, dashed stroke = stopped. Applied to five states with no exceptions.
  • Red reserved for the act — #E2564A appears only on the club-side confirm (the warning strip and Place veto) and on REJECTED. A veto state is never red, because members did not reject the proposal.
  • Two-clock layout — stage clock and appeal window side by side. Not in the export; the live build shows only one.
  • Reason inside the confirmation — the club cannot place a veto without reading back the exact wording members will see, and the consequence list ends by telling them it will be published under their name.
  • Signature row — crest plate, Club Management · Barcelona, publication time. The export carries no attribution on a veto at all, which is the single biggest gap in the current flow: an unsigned veto is unanswerable.
  • Hold-released banner — the VETO OVERRULED state implied by the reject_veto_success notification but never designed.
  • All member-facing copy — every sentence in the notice, the rationale and the What this means for you card is written for this proposal. The live build has no equivalent.
  • Red hover tint — #E86A5F on Place veto, derived from #E2564A exactly as #A8EC33 is derived from #9BE222 in merits.html and idea-card.html.
  • Frame chrome — the 52 px top bar, and the 375 px labelled frames, which are documentation only.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Is the bar to overturn a veto 80% Support?

    targetPercentageRejectVeto is server configuration, so the real number is not in the checkout. We have used 80% throughout, quoted in the notice, the steps, the trust copy and the confirm dialog. It appears in member-facing copy in four places, so it needs to be right before build.

  2. How long is the appeal window?

    Also server config — ideaStageAppealVeto and votingStageAppealVeto. We have shown 30:00, counting down live, beside a stage clock frozen at 41:12. If the real window is measured in hours rather than minutes the two-clock pairing still works, but the MM:SS format and the 24 px digit size will change.

  3. Should a veto be signed by a named person?

    We attribute it to Club Management · Barcelona, matching the live strings. Our view is that a named officer would make the power far more accountable and the signature row is already built to take a portrait instead of a crest. This is a governance decision, not a design one.

  4. Do we keep the phrase "Idea to Reject the Veto"?

    It is the live build's term and the mechanic's real name, so we keep it in body copy. But as a button label it is opaque, so the primary action reads Appeal the veto. Confirm you are happy with the two coexisting — one as the product noun, one as the verb.

  5. Is the Participation Deposit really not charged on a veto?

    en.json says "This will be free when the Proposal is Vetoed, Cancelled or Ruled Out", so step 1 tells the member their deposit is not charged if they accept. If that is wrong it is the single most damaging sentence on the screen.

  6. Can a veto ever be placed on a completed vote?

    We have built the mid-stage case the code describes: the clock pauses, the count holds, and both resume on release. We have written the copy to say where the vote stood rather than the result, which is accurate for a paused count. If management can also veto a published result, that is a different screen and different copy, and we would need to design it.

  7. Is the hatch reserved for veto alone?

    We would like to lock it to held and nothing else — not loading, not disabled, not pending, not scheduled. Its force comes from meaning exactly one thing. If Alerts or Actions need a "paused" state, they should use this same treatment; if they need "waiting", they should not.
