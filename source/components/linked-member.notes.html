How an adult links an account for someone under 18: the list of linked members, the details form with its age gate, a consent step, and the hand-off to the third party that checks the identity document. Four frames, and the only place in the product where a member acts on a child's behalf.

What you're looking at

The flow that has to be right first time

Nobody under 18 can join SportsHeist on their own. An adult links the account, approves the identity check, and stays responsible for it until the child turns 18. That makes this the highest consequence flow in the product and the one with the least room for a guess: it collects a child's date of birth, sends a photograph of their identity document and a picture of their face to a third party, and asks a parent to consent to all of it.

Three things drive the design. First, a row has to be identifiable — the shipped list shows a name and nothing else, so two children with the same surname are indistinguishable, and one of the four rows here is deliberately a second Thanh Nguyen to prove the row now separates them. Second, the date of birth is the field the whole account hangs off, so it is gated live: the form works out the age as you type and says what kind of account that makes, rather than accepting anything and failing later. Third, there is a consent step, because there isn't one today.

Everything else follows from those. One field anatomy, one control height — 56 px — a step rail so a parent knows there are three stages and no more, and a hand-off strip that names the third party, shows its host, and draws it with a dashed edge and no fill so it visibly sits outside the app.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. None of the retention periods are confirmed.

    This is the most important item on the page. Frame 3 tells a parent three specific things: the document photograph and the selfie are held by the vendor for 30 days and then deleted, and SportsHeist never receives the images; the result — pass or fail, the date, and the document type — is kept for 7 years; and the name, date of birth and relationship are deleted 90 days after the account is unlinked or 90 days after the child turns 18 and takes it over. Those are defensible shapes drawn from ordinary identity-check practice. They are not checked against the vendor contract or against the SportsHeist privacy policy, and each one is a binding commitment made to a parent about a child's data. Every figure needs verifying against both documents before this ships, and the third panel needs to match whatever the privacy policy already says.

  2. Is the third party nameable, and is that the production host?

    We name GBG as the provider and identify.idscan.cloud as the page, twice each, before the jump. The shipped flow names no third party at all — the vendor's host first appears in an operating-system camera prompt part-way through, which is the worst possible moment for a parent to meet an unfamiliar domain. Naming it up front is the fix, but it needs three confirmations: that the contract permits us to name the vendor to members, that the host is right for production rather than a test environment, and that the stated document types and the 7.5 MB limit are the vendor's real constraints.

  3. Is 5 the floor, and is 13 the posting age?

    The gate is built for ages 5 to 17 with a hard reject on either side, and the capability bands say under-13s can read but not post in Backchat and cannot send direct messages, while 13-to-17s can post under a display name with the guardian approving registration, spending and account changes. Both boundaries are the shapes that children's-data regimes tend to push you towards, but neither is a stated product rule, and the age floor in particular is a commercial decision as much as a legal one.

  4. Does the account really transfer at 18?

    The flow commits to it in three places: the list row counts down to the birthday, the capability panel says the account transfers with its Merits intact and the guardian's controls end that day, and the retention panel hangs its 90-day clock off the same event. It also promises an email to both parties a fortnight beforehand. As far as we can tell nothing in the build does any of this, so it is a feature this flow is describing rather than reflecting. If the transfer works differently — or does not exist — three panels and a countdown all change.

  5. How do we actually establish guardianship?

    The form asks for a relationship from a six-option list and the hint says we may ask you to evidence this before the membership is issued. There is no mechanism behind that sentence, and the identity check verifies the child's document, not the adult's claim to be their parent. That is the gap in the flow: everything else can be tightened by design, but proving guardianship needs a process decision. Related, and smaller: withdrawing consent is stated to close the account, and a failed check is stated to allow one retry with a different document. Both are our reading, and both are the sort of limit a parent will hit at the worst moment.
