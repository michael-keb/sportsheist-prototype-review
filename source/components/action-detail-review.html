What is real and what is proposed

Real
The verdict, the subject, the reason, every date and the vote count. All of it is already on the record MProposal returns from GET /proposals/{uuid}/ — including parent_id, the link back to the originating idea, which nothing in the live build reads.
Missing
Follow-through does not exist in the data. No owner, no due date, no completion flag, no evidence, and no endpoint to record any of them. The one step this screen cannot fill is the one the screen is for, so it is drawn as an unresolved dashed mark rather than faked as a progress bar.
Proposed
Three fields, marked Proposed wherever they appear: owner, committed_by and confirmed_at. Add those three and the loop closes.

States · five of the seven verdicts reach this screen

Reaches
Approved · Ruled out · Vetoed · Veto hold · Cancelled. An action exists because a proposal passed, so the verdict here is either the pass or something that struck it out afterwards.
Cannot
Rejected ends at the Result — a proposal members voted down never becomes an action. Lapsed ends at the Idea stage, before there is a proposal at all. Neither is drawn here; a link to either should land on the Result detail instead.
Live build
Ships two: rule_outed in red, everything else in green. And func.dart::findStatusAction matches case "rule_outed " with a trailing space, so an approved action currently renders to members as Ruled out, in red.

What you're looking at

Where the promise is kept, or isn't

SportsHeist's whole pitch is that members decide and the club follows through. Every other screen in the library builds up to that: an idea gathers likes, becomes a proposal, gets voted on, gets a result. This is the screen after the result — the one where a member finds out whether any of it mattered. It has had no design until now. The Actions card was rebuilt in results-actions.html; the detail behind it, a 505-line file on its own route (CRoute.actionDetail, referenced five times), was missed.

The screen reads top to bottom as one argument. The verdict — what the club decided, in the settled seven-word vocabulary. The subject — what was actually proposed, at full length. The instruction — what the club committed to doing about it, and who is accountable. Follow-through — six dated steps, five of which the data can prove. How it got here — the idea, the vote with its real counts, the result, and one plain sentence: this is happening because 1,079 of the club's 1,284 members voted for it. Then the discussion, because an action going quiet is the thing members will want to talk about.

The one thing this screen cannot do is the thing it exists for. There is no field anywhere in the app for whether an action was carried out — no owner, no due date, no completion flag, no evidence, and no endpoint to record any of it. So the last step of the timeline is drawn as an unresolved dashed mark reading Carried out · Not confirmed, rather than faked as a progress bar. Three fields close the loop and they are named in the Decisions section. Until they exist, the accountability loop is open by construction, not by accident.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused, unchanged

  • Verdict bar — results-actions.html, at its --lg detail scale. Same four tones, same two hues, same seven words.
  • The hold hatch — veto.html via the parent. Same 5px / 6px rhythm, same rgba(155,226,34,.30) ring, same ⊘, still static.
  • Comment thread — comments.html's .thread, .cm, .sig, club row, crest tile and CLUB badge.
  • Empty, error and skeleton panels — states.html's .empty, .state, .tile, .ill, .bone.
  • Clocks, count readout, threshold bar, numbered steps, attribution row — veto.html.
  • Club crest plate — the canonical 40px fallback form from club-selection.html.
  • Hero, section container, status pill, icon button, card shell, type chip — merits.html / merit-detail.html / idea-card.html.
  • Content — the same action the parent's first Actions card shows, so the card and its detail are the same object.

New in this proposal

  • The follow-through timeline — six steps, five built from timestamps already on the record. New composition, no new tokens.
  • The unrecorded mark — a 1.6px dashed #9BE222 ring and a dashed accent connector, for the one step no field can prove. Drawn under states.html's existing dashed-outline rule, deliberately not the hatch.
  • The Proposed tag — a hairline 19px chip, no hue, marking on-screen every value that has no field behind it.
  • The provenance chain — Idea → Vote → Result → Action, with the real counts and the threshold marked. Nothing in the live build reads parent_id, so no chain exists today.
  • The accountability sentence — one plain line naming the number of members who caused this. New copy.
  • Three proposed fields — owner, committed_by, confirmed_at. Everything on this screen tagged Proposed maps to one of them.
  • Bottom action bar — the live Action detail has no bottom chrome at all.
  • Discussion on an action — the endpoint supports it; the live screen omits it.
  • Real 4-line clamp, and the null-description empty — both are bug fixes as much as design.
  • .screen bottom padding at 128px — a departure from the contract's 110px, to clear the pinned bar. states.html took the same liberty at 176px.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Can the club record that an action was carried out?

    This is the only question on the page that matters. Nothing in the app can express follow-through: MProposal has no owner, no due date, no completion flag, no evidence and no confirmation timestamp, and there is no endpoint to set any of them. The only valueProgress in the repository sits on three dead mock classes (MAction, Result, Voting) that have no fromJson and are never fetched, hard-defaulted to 0.5. task_id is a scheduler handle, not a piece of work. So the product can currently tell a member what was decided and never what was done — the accountability loop it is sold on cannot be closed.

    We have not invented a tracker. We have designed the honest minimum: a timeline of the five steps the timestamps prove, and a sixth drawn as an unresolved dashed mark reading Carried out · Not confirmed. Three fields close it — owner, committed_by and confirmed_at — plus one write endpoint for the club. Confirm those and we will draw the confirmed state; refuse them and the dashed mark is the permanent truth of the screen, which is worth knowing before build.

  2. Who is accountable for an action — and can a member see it?

    We have shown FC Barcelona · club media against the crest plate. There is no field for it. The member who raised the idea is on the record (author_name, author_avatar) and appears in the byline, but the member is not the person who carries it out — the club is. If accountability is going to be per department, per named officer, or simply "the club", we need to know which, because it changes whether owner is a string, an enum or a user reference.

  3. status carries a verdict at one stage and an instruction at another. Split it?

    At the result stage status holds approved / rejected / vetoed / rule_outed. At the action stage it holds let_push_it (Publish it!) or let_do_it (Let's do it!) — those are instructions, not verdicts. One field cannot be both, which means an action's verdict is not stored anywhere: this screen has to infer Approved from the fact that a proposal reached stage: action at all. We have drawn it that way — verdict in the bar, instruction in its own panel — but the clean fix is a second field. And nobody has told us the difference between Publish it! and Let's do it!; we assumed Publish it! here because the parent's card did.

  4. There is no "against" count. Is 84% support really derivable?

    The record carries exactly two figures: voted and total_member_participants. There is no field for votes against and no field for abstentions, so 84% can only mean votes for, over the whole membership — it cannot mean share of votes cast. We have written it as 1,079 of 1,284 members so the denominator is explicit rather than implied. If a member can vote against and that is counted, the field is missing; if the threshold is a share of turnout rather than of membership, every percentage in the library is wrong.

  5. Is the threshold 70%, and does it move by type?

    We show 70%, marked on the bar rather than merely stated. Thresholds in the club config carries four separate values — positions, on-field, off-field and management decisions — plus a fifth for rejecting a veto. If they differ, the figure and the mark both have to read from config per type, and inherited from the parent: the same question is open on results-actions.html's chart.

  6. parent_id is an integer, but routing needs a uuid.

    The chain back to the originating idea is the point of the Provenance block, and parent_id is already on every record — nothing in the live build reads it. But navigation is by uuid (GET /proposals/{uuid}/), and parent_id is an int, so Open the idea cannot currently be wired without either a parent_uuid or an id-based lookup. Related: the counts on a record are current-stage only, so the idea's like count is not retrievable from the action payload. We have shown dates and authorship on the idea row rather than fabricate a like count.

  7. Does a ruled-out or vetoed action cost the member anything?

    We have followed the parent and the live string table: the deposit is forfeited only when an idea lapses, and proposalPage.free when proposal vetoed says outright "This will be free when the Proposal is Vetoed, Cancelled or Ruled Out". Every state that can reach this screen therefore reads Deposit already returned · 25 Credits. But the taxonomy tooltip contradicts it: CLString.listExplainTitleIDea[0] tells members on-field decisions "may be Vetoed or Ruled Out, and deposit forfeited, when it is not executable". One of those two strings is wrong and members are reading both. The 25 itself remains invented — no pricing exists in the repo.

  8. Two build bugs decide whether any of this is visible.

    Inherited from the parent and worth repeating because they land hardest here. func.dart::findStatusAction matches case "rule_outed " with a trailing space while the enum emits it without one, so every status that is not let_do_it or let_push_it falls through to the default and renders as Ruled out, in the danger colour — an approved action is currently shown to members as rejected. findTypeAction has the same trailing space on case "on_field_decisions ". Separately, is_danger is declared on MProposal and in its constructor but never parsed in fromJson, so it is always null — if it was meant to flag anything on this screen, it does not.

  9. Should the discussion live on the Action detail at all?

    We have put it there. The comments endpoint is keyed by proposal rather than by stage, so the data already supports it, and an action going quiet is exactly what members will want to argue about. The live screen shows no comments. If the thread should stay on the voting detail only, say so — the section lifts out cleanly and the bottom bar loses its purpose with it.

  10. Two open library-wide decisions apply here.

    Control height. The pinned bar's button is 52px, following forms.html; auth.html measured 64px off the Figma button component. Tap targets. Every 38px and 28px control on this screen carries the contract's ::after hit-area expansion to 44px, which is the pattern we recommend adopting library-wide rather than inflating the sampled visual sizes.
