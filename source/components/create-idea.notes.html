The flow a member uses to post an idea into the War Room: write it, check it against the club's rules and set its clocks, then lock a deposit to publish it. Five frames — compose, compliance and timing, the deposit dialog, the posted state, and the failure.

What you're looking at

The one flow that costs the member something

Posting an idea is not free. The member locks a deposit that is returned in full if the idea reaches Proposal and forfeited if it lapses, and once it posts it cannot be edited, paused or deleted. That makes this the most consequential flow in the product, and it is why so much of the design work here is about legibility of consequence rather than layout.

Four frames correspond to designed screens and are rebuilt in the dark palette. The success state is new: nothing in the capture set records what a member sees after a successful post, which is the one screen every member will see most often.

Three conventions were set in this file and are worth reading as decisions rather than styling. One currency name is used everywhere — Credits, the wallet's noun, at one figure, 25, which is also the wallet's. Machine-written and member-written content are never styled alike, and an on-screen key states the rule. And the consequence sentence is the most legible thing on the screen — white 15.5/700 on #1C2129 at about 16.2:1, where the old build set the same sentence in amber on maroon at about 2.6:1 in the smallest type in the region.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Four of the five frames — compose, compliance and timing, the deposit dialog and the failure dialog are designed screens, rebuilt here in the dark palette
  • The flow — three steps, a deposit confirmation, and a modal failure
  • Tokens — surfaces, hairlines, accent, semantic red, radii and the type scale, copied verbatim
  • Section container — #080A0C, 1 px --line, R16, 12 px padding, holding a pill and a circular control
  • Status pill, icon button, eyebrow — geometry and type unchanged
  • Idea card — the posted preview reuses the shell, gauge, countdown and tag from the built idea card
  • Row list — --line-soft top borders with none on the first
  • Photography — the member portrait comes from the export set

New in this proposal

  • The posted frame — no success screen exists anywhere in the capture set, and it is the screen a member will see most
  • One currency name — Credits, always with a figure in front of it; deposit survives only as a verb and as a state of Credits. Adopted from wallet.html rather than competed with
  • AI provenance as a design system — dashed-on-sunken for machine, solid-on-surface for member, an AI badge, a sentence-level dotted underline in the draft, an on-screen key stating the rule, and a count of how much of the draft is the assistant's
  • The AI estimate block — labelled as a model output, with its sample size and an explicit statement that it does not gate posting
  • Consequence callout — white 15.5/700 on #1C2129 at about 16.2:1, replacing amber on maroon at about 2.6:1 in the smallest type in the region
  • Consent as a gate — a 56 px checkbox row naming the amount, which enables the primary action
  • Button convention — secondary left, primary right, every control ≥ 48 px, affirmatives that name their action, and the same words for the same commitment in the form and the dialog
  • One dialog shell, one backdrop — the live build ships three modals with two backdrop treatments and three card widths
  • Timeline — the three clocks drawn as one track, so posting, voting and result are legible as a sequence
  • Explicit date and zone — Now — 2 Jun 2026, 10:53 with the zone named; the live build renders 06/02/2026 10:53 with neither the order nor the zone stated
  • The failure screen rewritten — the current OOPS! modal pipes a raw HTTP 403 (You do not have permission to access this resource.) into a card with no cause, no next step and no dismiss control
  • Copyable references — an idea reference on success, an error reference on failure
  • Focus states — visible keyboard focus on every control; nothing in the frames shows one

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. What is the currency called?

    One concept currently has five names. The Merits tab calls it Merits, the API calls it Reputation Points, and this flow's own strings use Credits, Participation Credits, Participation Deposit and a legacy BOND — with one string shipping the literal [x] PARTICIPATION CREDITS, the placeholder never substituted. This file now uses Credits throughout as the unit, always with a figure in front of it, with deposit in lower case for what happens to them rather than a second noun. That is wallet.html's resolution; this file originally proposed Participation Credits and has been brought onto the wallet's version, because the wallet is the screen where the noun has to carry a balance. BOND is retired. Pick one name and we will use it everywhere, including the Merits tab.

  2. What is the third category actually called?

    The category enum has three values and only two have display names. The third is rule_outed — with a trailing space in the source literal — and no label for it exists anywhere in the build. We have guessed Rule change from the identifier. That is a guess on a control that determines what rules an idea is judged against, so it needs a real name.

  3. Settled: forfeited on lapse, and on lapse only.

    The whole flow is built around it: returned in full at 10:59 if the idea reaches Proposal, forfeited if it lapses. Every warning on the screen says so and the consent checkbox makes the member agree to it in words. That reading is now the one the whole set uses. The wallet used to promise the deposit always comes back and the Results screen used to show Ruled out taking it; both have been brought onto this file's rule, because the live string table backs it — ideaPage.agreement refunds only when the Idea becomes a Proposal, notification.idea_lapsed forfeits on lapse, and proposalPage.free when proposal vetoed confirms a Proposal that is Vetoed, Cancelled or Ruled Out costs the member nothing more.

    Two things we changed here rather than left standing. The three deposit lines quoted 11:02, which is the result time; the deposit actually resolves at 10:59, when the Ideas stage ends and the idea either becomes a Proposal or lapses. And the confirmation now says outright that nothing which happens to the Proposal afterwards can take the deposit back. What we still need: confirmation that forfeiture is total rather than partial, and that it is never reversed on appeal.

  4. Is 25 the deposit for every idea, and where does 25 come from?

    It comes from us. No pricing of any kind exists in the repo — not in the string table, not in config, not in the API models — so the figure is invented and needs a product decision before build. This flow originally showed 50 while the wallet showed 25.0, which meant a client reading both screens saw two prices for one action. We settled on 25 because the wallet's ledger arithmetic already derives from it — 300.0 on deposit across 12 live ideas is 25.0 each, and its running-balance column reconciles at 25 — so 25 was the only figure with anything behind it. The wallet writes it 25.0 under its own always-one-decimal rule; this flow writes 25 in whole Credits. Same number, two formats, and if you would rather one format won everywhere, say which.

    The amount is 25 everywhere in this flow, against a balance of 340. Nothing indicates whether the deposit varies by category, by club, by the length of the voting window a member chooses, or with a member's standing. If it varies, the amount has to be derived and shown before the member sets the clocks, not after.

  5. How much of an idea may the assistant write?

    The build assumes the answer is "all of it, as long as the member can see which parts", so provenance travels with the idea at sentence level and the draft counts how much came from the model. What it does not answer is whether that provenance is visible to other members when the idea appears in the War Room. If a fully machine-written idea should be labelled publicly, that is a change to the idea card, not to this flow.

  6. Should the AI estimate be shown at all?

    The block reports a 75% chance of reaching Proposal from 214 comparable ideas, and says in its own footnote that it is an estimate and does not affect whether the idea may post. It is still a number that will discourage people from posting. It is built to be removable in one block if you would rather it were not there.

  7. Are the timing windows really minutes?

    The controls read 6 minutes for collecting likes and 3 minutes for the vote, needing 120 likes and passing at 80% yes. Nine minutes from post to result, with a 120-like bar, is a very short fuse for a club decision — and the War Room cards show countdowns of hours. Confirm the ranges a member may choose from, because the timeline drawing depends on them.
