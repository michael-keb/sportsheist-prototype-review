The live text lane that runs beside the audio while a locker room is open. A member reads it with one thumb while somebody else is talking, types two words, and never comes back to it — which is why it is built nothing like the Comments thread that hangs off a proposal.

What you're looking at

The third talking surface, and the fastest one

SportsHeist now has three places where members say things, and they are easy to confuse. Locker room is the audio stage — hands up, mics, speakers, reactions, and nobody types. Comments is the argument attached to a War Room proposal — a bordered panel of rows, threaded one level, sortable, with agree and disagree counts, meant to be re-read the day a vote closes. This screen is neither. It is the chat lane inside a room that is already live: ephemeral, fast, dozens of short messages, read once and in the order they arrived.

Four rules keep it from drifting into either of the others. Order is the only structure — no replies, no indent, no sort control, because a chat you can re-order is a comment thread with a worse composer. Nothing is counted — Comments carries agreement as ink, chat does not carry agreement at all. Bubbles on the canvas, not rows in a panel, because a bubble's width tells you a message is two words long before you read it. And one accent fill on the whole screen — the send button, exactly as in Comments, where it means "post" and nothing else.

The screen is a proposal in full. The four kinds of author, the grouping rule, the system lines, the unread divider, the jump-to-latest control, the typing indicator, every send state and the moderation states are all new; see Provenance for the line-by-line split.

New in this proposal

  • The whole screen. chat_body.dart is the Backchat home scaffold — two 20%-opacity background PNGs, the shared hero with an AddNewRoom button, and a pull-to-refresh list of Fan Stadium plus live and upcoming rooms. There is no message list, no composer, no message model, no send call and no chat socket anywhere in the repository; SocketIO carries only like and voted proposal events. The bubbles, composer, send and emoji controls in the Backchat atlas (BC-C60–BC-C64) exist only in the BC-03/BC-04 screenshots, which the atlas records as not located — no counterpart anywhere in lib/.
  • Author distinction of any kind. The shipped bubble is a single #542BE2 pill with no author, avatar, timestamp, in/out variant or max width. Own-versus-others, host, club and system lines are all new.
  • Consecutive-message grouping and the two-minute run rule — new
  • Join / leave system lines, batched at 30 seconds and capped at two names — new
  • Unread divider — new
  • Jump to latest, and the rule that the log only auto-scrolls when already at the foot — new
  • Typing indicator with names — new
  • Sending, failed, Retry and Delete — new. The shipped send button has no disabled rendering at all.
  • Character limit, counter and the growth cap — new; there is no limit or counter on the shipped composer
  • Return sends, Shift+Return newlines — new
  • Reconnecting and muted composer states — new
  • Removed-by-a-moderator line, and the overflow as the only route to Report — new here, extending the moderation set comments.html proposed
  • Empty state — new; there is no empty chat anywhere in the capture set
  • 14 px bubble radius and the 4 px tail corner — the only radii in the file outside the kit's three. The shipped bubble is R≈36, a full pill at one line, which reads as a control rather than as speech.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Is there a text chat at all, and who runs it?

    This is the first question, because nothing in the Flutter app sends a message. The room runtime that produced BC-03 and BC-04 sits outside the repository — no RTC dependency, no room page, and a font family that appears nowhere else in the product — so the bubbles in those screenshots are almost certainly a third-party SDK's own UI. If that stays true, this design is a specification for a surface the SDK vendor would have to be able to skin, and the practical answer may be that the chat is built in-house against the existing socket instead. We need to know which before build.

  2. Is the chat ephemeral or kept?

    Everything here assumes ephemeral — the log lives as long as the room does and is gone when it closes. That assumption drives the absence of search, of day dividers, of permalinks and of a "load earlier" control. If chat is retained and readable after the room ends, it needs all four, plus a decision about whether a member can delete their own history.

  3. Who can post, and is there a rate limit?

    The audio stage has hands-up and a speaker list, so a room already has a permission model for talking. We have assumed every attendee can type immediately, with no slow mode and no listener-only lock. A 200-person room during a derby will need at least a host-controlled slow mode; the composer's strip has a slot reserved for it, but the rule is not designed.

  4. Who moderates, and how fast?

    We have proposed a mute with an end time and an appeal, and a removed-message line, on the same reasoning as Comments — a product with members under 18 cannot ship a live unmoderated chat. But a live surface needs someone acting in seconds, not the next working day. Does the host moderate their own room, does the club, or is there a SportsHeist queue? We have assumed the host can mute inside their own room and the club moderates removals.

  5. Are reactions and emoji in or out?

    The captures show a floating reaction layer and a ~54pt emoji button inside the composer, and locker-room.html already rebuilt reactions as a drawn tray on the audio surface. We have left both out of the chat so that reactions mean one thing in one place. If an emoji picker goes into the composer, it needs a drawn glyph set — the contract permits no system emoji, and the shipped 🙏 is a platform glyph the brand cannot recolour.

  6. Does 14 px body text stand?

    The contract's measured scale puts body copy at 13/400, and Comments sets comment text at 13px. Chat bubbles here are 14px — the document's own body size — on the grounds that a live surface is read at a glance, one-handed, while listening to something else. It is a deliberate one-step increase and we would rather have it signed off than discovered.

  7. Should the room's unread total appear on the Backchat tab?

    The Stage / Chat switch carries the room's unread count. Nothing yet decides whether that number also escapes the room — onto the Backchat tab badge, or into Alerts. We have assumed it does not: a chat that pushes a badge for every message trains members to ignore the badge.
