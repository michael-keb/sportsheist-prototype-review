Tab 1 of 5 and the app's home screen. Four rails stacked in lifecycle order — Ideas, Voting, Results, Actions — so a member can see everything the club is deciding, everything it has decided, and what is being done about it, in one scroll. Four screens: the hub, and the three states it passes through before it gets there.

What you're looking at

A rebrand, not a redesign

War Room is one of only two tabs that already exist as designed frames in the Figma file. This build reproduces those frames in the dark palette: the hero, the four rails, the card shells and the card interiors are all drawn in the export and are rebuilt here at measured values rather than reinterpreted. What changed is colour and surface, not structure.

The four rails are the product's whole lifecycle. An Idea collects likes against a bar; if it clears the bar it becomes a Proposal and moves to the Voting rail; the vote produces a Result; the result produces an Action with an owner. Each rail is the same shape — a header with controls, a divider, a horizontal carousel of 268 px cards — so the four stages read as one machine.

Everything in this file is live: the countdowns tick, the gauges and meters draw when a card scrolls into view, and the Like control toggles. That is deliberate — the mechanic is the point of the screen, and a static screenshot hides it.

Why four phones. The hub used to be shown as one screen, which only documented the happy path. It is now four frames, because the tab reaches three other states before it reaches that one. Frame 02 is the loading skeleton the build already has, redrawn dark. Frame 03 is a club with nothing running — four empty rails, each carrying the app's own string. Frame 04 is a failed load, and it is the one screen here with no code behind it: WarRoomStatus.fails is declared and raised twice, and nothing in the app ever tests for it, so a failure currently renders as frame 03. A member whose request died is told their club has nothing going on. That is the single most consequential finding on this tab.

Three things in the current build were measured and are fixed here rather than carried over. The live hero prints its title straight over the club crest with no scrim, which measures about 1.9:1 on the largest text in the product; the crest is gone from this hero and the title now measures 10.6:1 at the lightest point of the green field and 13.5:1 at the darkest. Card titles in the live build read Auto Position Proposal 1776537160301 — a raw epoch-millisecond id — so every title here is clamped to two lines and will degrade instead of tearing the card apart. And the live chart panel prints Current: 1 votes; every count on this screen is rendered from the number, so the singular case is built as a state you can see rather than a bug waiting to reappear.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • The screen — War Room is a designed frame; this is a reproduction in the dark palette, not a new layout
  • Hero banner — height, gradient angle and stops, field triangles, acid peaks, prop-in-front layering
  • Four rails in lifecycle order — Ideas → Voting → Results → Actions, and the header controls on each
  • Card geometry — 268 px, 16 px padding, R16, taken from the Idea card
  • Idea card interior — gauge ring, countdown, type tag and both Like states
  • Approve / reject standing — the Voting card's job, and the Vote here action
  • Verdict bar — a full-width filled band with a circular glyph and a caps label, under the card title
  • Content and behaviour — the rails, the CTA words, the countdown format and the lifecycle come from WR-01, WR-09 and WR-10
  • Tab bar — 58 px pill, active-as-accent-with-label, four outline icons
  • Photography — all six portraits and the athlete cutout come from the exports
  • Surfaces, radii, type scale — sampled, not approximated
  • Loading shape — frame 02 follows the shipped shimmer's own structure, including its three-rail count
  • Empty-rail copy — all four headings on frame 03 are the app's strings, verbatim

New in this proposal

  • Rail divider — the fading acid hairline over a bloom. The export's violet lens flare has no equivalent in this palette, so it is re-read rather than recoloured
  • No crest behind the title — the crest is out of the hero, which is what takes the title from about 1.9:1 to 10.6:1
  • Approve / reject as a linear meter — the export draws a semicircular dial with a white needle and an unlabelled painted arc. Two encodings on one gauge, neither labelled; this states the share, the reject share and the threshold in words and figures
  • Stated threshold — Threshold 70% and a tick on the bar. The export shows the bar's position but never names it
  • Rejected bar without a tint — a red ring on the panel ground instead of a red fill, because the tinted version measures 3.84:1
  • Veto-hold verdict — the hub export has approved and rejected only. Drawn with the hold hatch settled in veto.html rather than a neutral ring, so a hold looks the same here as it does there
  • Two-line title clamp and reserved title lines — protects the card from the build's auto-generated titles and lines the rails up
  • Vote-count pluralisation — the count is rendered from the number so 1 vote cast cannot regress
  • Gauge and meter draw-in, ticking countdowns — the frames are static strings
  • Turnout in the eyebrow — moved off the footer, which truncated at 268 px when it carried both the share and the count
  • Focus states — visible keyboard focus on every in-card action, absent from the frames
  • Dropped: the MORE → rail-end card — the header's View all ↗ pill does that job, and the export carried both
  • Dropped: the full-width + NEW IDEA pill — folded into the accent + circle in the Ideas header
  • Copy fixes — 35% of Likes required loses its mid-sentence capital; Manage… sets in full
  • Dark skeleton — the shipped shimmer is Colors.grey.shade300 → shade100, a light-theme skeleton running on a dark screen. Redrawn on the States kit's bone
  • Empty rails as a screen — the build draws a bordered box holding one line of text. This is the States kit's panel with a drawn illustration and one line saying what fills the rail. The supporting sentence under each heading is new
  • The whole load-failed frame — WarRoomStatus.fails has no branch anywhere in the app. Both sentences are new, modelled on the one failure string the product does have, in Profile
  • Four working rail filters — one sheet per rail, each on the axes that rail's own cards carry

All four filter glyphs are live. Each was an aria-hidden icon on a button with no handler. Each now opens the library's standard bottom sheet, filters only its own rail, and leaves a count and a dot on the glyph so an active filter is visible after the sheet closes. The axes differ per rail because the cards differ: an Idea has a kind and a like, a Proposal has a kind and a position against its threshold, a Result and an Action have a verdict. Within a group any tick matches; across groups all must — so Management plus Below threshold narrows rather than widens.

Every axis is read back off the card at the moment the filter runs — the tag it prints, the pressed state of its own Like button, the --approve and --th pair on its meter, the verdict modifier on its plate. Nothing is duplicated into a second attribute, so a filter cannot drift from what the card is drawing. That also means the two controls interact honestly: with the Ideas rail filtered to Ideas I have liked, un-liking the last matching card empties the rail in front of you. The empty rails frame drops the filter glyph entirely, because there is nothing to filter.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Which hero object belongs to War Room?

    Only one prop asset exists — the knockout athlete cut from the Get Started group shot — so every hero in the set currently reuses the same figure. The Figma War Room hero carries a helmet, and we have used the athlete in its place because drawing a pseudo-3D helmet by hand would be worse than no object. Either supply the helmet cutout, or commit to figure-based cutouts on all five tabs and we will vary the crop per tab.

  2. Should the club crest come back?

    The live hero prints WAR ROOM straight over the FC Barcelona crest with no scrim, which measures about 1.9:1 — the worst contrast in the product, on its largest text. We removed the crest rather than scrim it, so the hero now carries no club identity at all. If the crest has to be there, the canonical crest plate at 64 px beside the title is the way to do it — measured, and constant for every club — but it changes the hero lockup and we would rather you chose.

  3. Is the yes bar 70% or 80%?

    Every voting card here shows Threshold 70%, while the idea body copy on the first card says the hire only counts if the vote clears the 80% yes bar. Both came from the source material. We built 70% as the displayed threshold because it is the value drawn on the meter. If the bar varies by category or by stage, the card needs to say which bar applies.

  4. Are there two tag vocabularies or one?

    Ideas are tagged Decision and Position. Proposals on the very next rail are tagged Management and Off-field. That reads as two taxonomies on the same card shape — one describing what kind of thing an idea is, one describing its subject area. We rendered both in the same accent tag. If both are real they should look different; if only one is real, the other should go.

  5. What does a veto show, and should a hold sit in Results at all?

    The vetoed card carries a turnout but no percentage, and reads Held · appeal open. We assumed a veto suppresses the outcome figure, because the figure is no longer what decided it. If members are entitled to see how the vote actually went before it was held, that is a different card.

    We also read this card's own copy as a hold rather than a concluded veto — it says the board is holding it and the appeal is open — so it now carries the hold hatch settled in veto.html. A veto that has run out its appeal window is a different thing: that one is finished, and it is drawn in red on the Results screen, not hatched. Confirm the hub rail only ever shows the live, still-appealable case; if a concluded veto can appear here too, this rail needs both treatments and the eyebrow has to say which is which.

  6. Where do the Action verdict strings come from?

    Results read Approved, Rejected, Vetoed. Actions read Publish it! and Ruled out — a different register, and the only exclamation mark, in the same bar shape. We kept them verbatim because they are the strings in the source, but they should either join the results vocabulary or be defined as a separate set with its own rules.

  7. Rail header controls at 34 px or 38 px?

    The contract puts a circular icon button at 38 px and the View all ↗ pill at 34 px. In a rail header the two sit on one line, so we matched the circles to the pill at 34 px, which is also what the Idea card component does. Confirm that, or we will raise the pill to 38 px everywhere instead — the one thing we should not ship is two heights in one row.

  8. What does an empty rail say?

    Answered by the code, and frame 03 now uses its words. Each rail has its own empty branch, but they draw on two templates — No {} added yet for Ideas and Voting, No {} yet for Results and Actions — which is why the Voting rail reads No voting added yet. Nobody adds a voting. We kept it verbatim rather than quietly correcting it, because it is the string a member sees today, but it should become No votes open yet or the four rails should share one template. The second line under each heading is ours: the shipped box says only that the rail is empty, never what would fill it.

  9. Should a failed load look different from an empty club?

    This is the biggest gap on the tab and it needs a product decision, not a design one. WarRoomStatus.fails exists and is raised twice, and nothing anywhere tests for it — the screen branches on inProcess and treats everything else as success. So when the request dies, the hub renders against the empty lists it started with, and the member is told their club has no ideas, no votes, no results and no actions. A failure is displayed as an accurate-looking report that the club is doing nothing.

    Frame 04 is what we think should be there instead: one full-screen panel with a retry, replacing all four rails. The alternative worth considering is a per-rail failure, since the hub makes two calls and one can fail while the other succeeds — but the cubit collapses both into a single status, so per-rail would need the state model changed first. Confirm which, and the words: both sentences on frame 04 are ours.
