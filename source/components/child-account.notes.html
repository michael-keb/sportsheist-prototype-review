The whole child-account area, in two groups. Frames 01–17 are a proposal — the hub, the seven-step flow for adding a child, the stored record in three states, and the two-step route for closing an account. Frames 18 and 19 are the app today: a list and a dead dialog, which is the entire feature in code. Every screen here is used by the parent — the child never sees any of them. It is the largest uncovered area in the product and the only one whose subject is a minor.

What you're looking at

Two screens ship. Seventeen do not.

Read this paragraph before you click anything. Frames 18 and 19, at the end, are the child-account feature as it exists in the app right now: a list of names, each row labelled EDIT, a line telling you to finish on a desktop browser, and a confirmation dialog wired to nothing. That is all of it — one file, lib/pages/profile/child_account/child_account_screen.dart, 193 lines. Frames 01–17 are a design proposal. No screen in that flow exists in code, no route reaches it, and no API supports it. A parent cannot add or verify a child account in the app today, and nothing in frames 01–17 should be demonstrated as though they can. The two shipped frames are marked SHIPPED in their caption and carry a banner saying so on the frame itself.

Every screen here is one a parent or legal guardian uses to act on behalf of someone under 18. They give us the child's name and date of birth, they set up how the child signs in, they choose the club, they pay the fee, and they consent to a third party photographing the child's passport. The child appears throughout as a subject and never as a user.

Which makes the single biggest finding worth stating first: the shipped flow has no consent step at all. Across all seventeen captured screens there is no consent checkbox, no legal link, no privacy statement, no retention period and no accepted-document list. The one screen that looks like consent, CA-13, asks "Do you agree to an ID check for your child to keep Sports Heist fair?" and then renders one affirmative button and no way to say no. It names no third party either — the vendor's hostname identify.idscan.cloud first reaches the parent inside an iOS camera prompt on the screen after. Frame 10 is the screen that was missing, and frames 11 and 12 are the hand-off done in the open.

The second finding is that the age gate does not exist. CA-06 — the only screen in a minors-only flow that collects a date of birth — accepted 02/06/2002, twenty-four years before the capture, with CONTINUE enabled and no message. The picker behind it (date.dart:109–116) has exactly two bounds available and both default to null; its strictest setting only means "not in the future". Frame 03 opens pre-filled with that same date, rejected, with the reason and the consequence on screen. Correct it to 02/09/2010 and it passes.

The third is about the password. CA-09 has the parent set the child's password — "Only you can control this password" — and then CA-01, CA-04 and CA-16 render nine mask glyphs in a field with no change control, which reads as a stored secret somebody could retrieve. Frame 06 makes sending the child a set-your-own link the default, and no screen in this component ever renders a password field for the record. The row reads Last changed with a Reset action instead.

What ships instead is frames 18 and 19. lib/pages/profile/child_account/child_account_screen.dart is 193 lines and is the only file under child_account/, routed at routes.dart:256. It renders a white screen (backgroundColor: CColor.white, line 38 — the light screen in a dark app) with a Bebas Neue header reading CHILD'S ACCOUNT, a body heading reading CREATE CHILD ACCOUNT that creates nothing, a list of children's names in caps each with the word EDIT under it, and a plain-text line reading "To add a child account, please proceed to Sports Heist on Desktop" (line 108, key go to web to add) — a Text, not a link, so tapping it does nothing. There is no avatar, no add control, no edit form, no search and no empty state. Tapping a row opens the frame-19 dialog reading ARE YOU SURE? / All changes will be lost. whose Yes and No buttons both just dismiss it — no handler is passed (lines 87–99, defaults at dialogs.dart:993–1024) — on a screen with nothing editable to lose. Meanwhile assets/translations/vi.json lines 84–140 hold fifty-seven lines of English-language child-account strings that en.json does not have — SET UP PASSWORD, EMAIL ADDRESS VERIFICATION, CLUB MEMBERSHIP ID, PARENT CONTROL, RESET AUTO TOP-UP, AGREE — every one of them with zero references in any .dart file. The Vietnamese translation file is where this feature was specified. Treat these frames as a design for something that has to be built, not a restyle of something that ships.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused, unchanged

  • Token block — copied verbatim from merits.html. No new hue: two colours plus #E2564A.
  • Field system — forms.html: one label convention, one 52px shell, the subgrid two-up, the phone-field divider and the track-accent switch.
  • Dialog shell and scrim — dialogs.html: 340px card, 46px pill action row, rgba(3,4,1,.72) at 10px blur.
  • Dialog-over-host staging — upload.html: .stg / .host / .ovl, the permission illustration, and the inline warning banner.
  • State panel and illustration rules — states.html: --section panel, and the three drawing rules (solid fill = context, accent wash + 1.8 stroke = exists, 5-5 dash = should exist and doesn't).
  • Stepper bars — onboarding.html, geometry and done/now logic untouched.
  • Crest plate — club-selection.html, at three of its three sizes, using its documented code fallback.
  • Row, age gate, consent panel and hand-off strip — linked-member.html, carried forward rather than re-argued.
  • Portraits — --av-04, --av-05, --av-07, --av-13 from _assets/avatars.css.
  • Frames 18–19 add nothing. They are a reproduction of child_account_screen.dart: its order, its structure and its strings, taken from the source and en.json. Frame 19 uses the dialogs.html shell unchanged; frame 18 adds one row class and one paragraph class and no new token.

New in this proposal

  • A consent screen — what is collected, who checks it, how long it is kept, three policy links, one required checkbox, a consent log line and a decline path. Nothing equivalent exists on any of the seventeen screens.
  • The age gate — a 5–17 range with a derived-age readout and five failure messages, on the one screen that collects a date of birth.
  • Row facts — age, date of birth, relationship, state and reference, so two identically named records read as two people.
  • Stepper caption — Step n of 7 plus the step's name, replacing seven labels that cannot fit the measure. This is an extension to onboarding.html, not a second progress idiom.
  • The external panel — dashed edge, host strip, EXTERNAL tag. New because nothing in the library had to render a surface we do not own.
  • The record list (.rec) — key/value rows with a per-row action, replacing four editable-looking fields that had no Save control. No password row.
  • Club cards with two states — registered and following-only — plus renewal date, amount and remaining club places.
  • Deletion consequences — four of them with figures, a 90-day / 2033 data statement, a required checkbox, and two softer alternatives (suspend, stop the renewal).
  • The turning-18 notice — stated on the row, on the record and in the retention copy.

But it is now a strict subset of a component that covers the same ground plus thirteen more screens, and keeping it means maintaining two entry points and, worse, two vocabularies: it calls the object a linked member, while the manifest, the specs, the code and en.json all call it a child account. Two names for one thing in one library is the exact defect this rebrand keeps correcting elsewhere. It should not be kept as the profile-tree entry point either: the entry point is frame 01 here, which is the same screen with more on it. We have not touched linked-member.html — the decision is yours. Frames 18–19 strengthen the case rather than weakening it: the screen that is actually wired into the profile tree today is frame 18, and it now lives in this component too, so both the shipped state and the proposed one sit in one place.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Are frames 18–19 marked clearly enough?

    They carry a SHIPPED tag in the caption and a banner on the frame, and they sit last so the proposal reads as a flow first. That is our judgement, and it is the only thing standing between a client and the belief that a parent can add and verify a child account in the app today. If this component is going to be demonstrated live, say so and we will move the two shipped frames to the front instead, so nobody reaches the proposal without passing them.

  2. Should the shipped frames be drawn in the shipped chrome?

    We drew them in the dark rebrand language, with the shipped values recorded in the anatomy table rather than reproducing the white screen, the Bebas Neue titles and the #542BE2 purple literally. The reason is the contract: two hues plus #E2564A, no webfonts, no third ground. The cost is that the single most jarring fact about the shipped screen — that it is a light screen in a dark app (child_account_screen.dart:38) — is a sentence in the notes rather than something you can see. If you would rather see it, we can add a screenshot-style plate; it should not be a live frame.

  3. Retire linked-member.html?

    Our recommendation, set out above, is retire it into this component and settle on child account as the name of the object across the library. If you want to keep linked member as the product term instead, say so and we will rename all seventeen frames — but one of the two has to go.

  4. What is the youngest age a child account can be?

    There is no age logic anywhere in the app to derive this from. We have gated 5 to 17, with reading-only behaviour under 13 and posting from 13. That is a defensible default, not a decision we can make: it is a legal question about the jurisdictions you operate in, and it changes what the ID check can even be asked to prove for a younger child who holds no document.

  5. Confirm the retention periods, then let us hard-code them.

    Frame 10 states 30 days for the document photo and selfie at GBG, 7 years for the pass/fail result, and 90 days after closure for name and date of birth. Every one of those is our estimate — no retention rule exists anywhere in the codebase, and no privacy copy exists to check them against. They are the most legally load-bearing numbers in the component and they need to come from your DPO, not from us.

  6. Who is allowed to open the child's inbox?

    CA-08's copy says the code goes to "your child's email" and the parent is the one entering it, so the shipped design either assumes the parent has the child's mailbox or assumes the child reads the code out. We have made the parent's own address an offered alternative on frame 04 and said out loud that someone has to open the inbox. Is a child-owned mailbox a requirement, or should the parent's address be the default?

  7. Does the parent ever set the child's password?

    We made sending the child a set-your-own link the default and kept parent-set as the secondary option for younger children, with a forced change at first sign-in. This matters because the shipped record screens displayed a masked password with no change control, which is either a display of something retrievable or a lie about what's stored. Confirm that no screen should ever render a password field for a child account.

  8. Consent before the fee, or after it?

    CA-13 sits after CA-12, so the parent agrees a $20 annual fee before being told what an identity check involves. We have kept the shipped order and made the fee screen state that nothing is charged until the check passes — but the honest sequence is consent first. Do you want them swapped?

  9. Is the GBG hand-off still the plan?

    There is no identity-verification integration in this snapshot at all: no webview_flutter, no flutter_inappwebview, zero matches for idscan anywhere in the repository, and the single occurrence of GBG is a comment at upload.dart:44 borrowing its 7.5 MB figure. Frames 11 and 12 are drawn on the assumption that GBG in an embedded web view is the intended design, because that is what the captures show. If the vendor or the mechanism has changed, those two frames change with it.

  10. The vendor page needs work we cannot do.

    GBG's page addresses the reader as the document holder throughout — your Identity Document — on a page photographing a 15-year-old, and it renders the SportsHeist lockup clipped to the single word Heist. It also reproduces our own copy, including the ungrammatical "This prevent fraud", which needs fixing on our side first. Who owns raising the addressee wording, the client-logo asset and the retention statement with GBG?

  11. Confirm the fee mechanics.

    We have stated $20 a year per club, charged when the check passes, not refunded if the account closes mid-season, and the club place not returned. The first two come from CA-12; the last two are ours, and they are the sentences a parent will hold you to. There is no payment code in the app to check them against — no Stripe SDK, no charge call, no card capture — so all of it is copy waiting on a commercial decision.

  12. What does a guest actually lose?

    CA-12 offers Continue as guest and says nothing about what the guest path excludes. We have written it as no club registration, no vote, no Participation Credits, following still allowed. Confirm, particularly the vote — it is the thing the whole membership rests on.

  13. Three defects to fix regardless of this design.

    The first two are drawn, in frames 18 and 19. One: tapping a child row opens ARE YOU SURE? / All changes will be lost. with no handler attached, so both buttons dismiss and the row does nothing (child_account_screen.dart:87–99, defaults at dialogs.dart:993–1024) — and the rows are labelled EDIT (:103). Two: any API failure shimmers indefinitely, because content renders only on success and everything else falls through to the skeleton (:73, :122, profile.dart:43, :48). We re-checked this on the current snapshot and it still holds: the error branch that landed in profile_body.dart:68 / :447 fixed the Profile tab and did not reach this screen. Porting that buildErrorState() across is the whole fix. Three: a child's emailVerifyToken is held on the client model and re-emitted by toJson() (models/src/user.dart:28–29, :97–98, :262–263) — a minor's verification token should not be round-tripping through the client. Separately, and outside this component's scope: a plaintext Auth0 client secret sits in .env.production:10 and both env files are declared as bundled assets at pubspec.yaml:109–110, so it ships inside the binary. Treat it as compromised and rotate it.
