What a member sees once a vote is over. The screen lists every settled proposal with its outcome, then the same cards again in an Actions framing that says what happens next. At its centre is one small component — the verdict bar — that has to carry seven different endings.

What you're looking at

Seven endings, two colours

A proposal can finish in more ways than the product currently admits. It can be approved or rejected by the members. It can be struck out by club management. It can turn out to be unexecutable. It can run out of time short of the like threshold, or be cancelled because the match ended. And while a veto is being appealed it is paused — decided by nobody yet.

None of that is designed in the Figma file. This screen proposes it, and the whole proposal rests on one component: a 56 px bar carrying a badge, a word and a line of figures. Every ending uses that same bar. What changes between them is the tone, and there are still only two colours: accent and semantic red. The third and fourth tones are not colours at all — a hold is drawn as the accent interrupted, static bars struck at 135°, and an ending that is simply over and quiet gets no hue.

That hatch is not ours: it was settled on the veto screens and this file copies its construction verbatim, down to the 5 px on / 6 px off spacing. It appears exactly once here, on Veto hold, and it is deliberately withheld from everything else — nothing loading, disabled, pending or scheduled gets it. Its force comes from meaning one thing: nothing is decided yet. Lapsed and Cancelled keep the quiet, hueless treatment, because those are finished — held is loud, finished is quiet.

The live War Room already has the right instinct here — its verdict bar is the one place in the app where a state is carried by an icon and a word and a colour, rather than colour alone. That principle is the only thing we kept. What we did not keep is the execution: the same positive outcome is currently drawn in four different greens — #1AA553 in the token file, #22C55E on the Results card, #1DBF5B on the Actions card and #99D0AB on the chart — and it is called APPROVED on one screen and PUBLISH IT! on the next. Here there is one accent green, one red, and one word per ending.

The other half of the screen is the result detail: a counting-round chart and a tally, for proposals settled by elimination rather than a single yes-or-no. The live version of that chart is a pure #000000 panel with an empty plot area, a 0–50 axis and no threshold drawn at all; this one sits on the contract's surfaces, has a real 0/50/100 axis, marks the threshold it had to clear, and emphasises the outcome. It draws its outcome line on scroll and holds; everything else is static.

States

What varies

The seven verdict readings are gathered on one screen (02) so they can be compared; in the product each one belongs to a single card in the results list (01). Result vs Action is also within that one list. Empty list is its own screen (04), and the chart states belong to result detail (03).

StateTreatment
ApprovedAccent tone, tick badge, 84% support · 70% needed · 1,079 votes.
RejectedRed tone, cross badge, the same three figures. Red always arrives with a glyph and a word, never on its own.
VetoedRed tone, but a ringed diagonal strike rather than a bare cross — the badge distinguishes a management block from a member rejection at the same tone. Red, not the hold hatch, because the sub-line says appeal closed: this veto is concluded, not paused. A veto that is still appealable is the separate Veto hold state below.
Ruled outRed tone, ringed minus badge. The vote passed but the instruction could not be carried out. The sub-line names the blocker and states that the deposit is already returned — it came back when the idea became a Proposal, so no verdict after that point can take it.
Veto holdThe held tone: static accent bars at 135° over #13171E, an rgba(155,226,34,.30) ring and the ⊘ glyph in accent. A live countdown — 00:22:00 ticking down in tabular figures, kept full-contrast white — makes it the only bar with a running clock. This replaces the hueless pause badge this file first shipped; the hatch is the settled treatment and this is the single place it is spent.
LapsedInert tone, hourglass badge, 61 of 120 likes · never reached the vote · deposit forfeited. Stays hueless: an idea that ran out of time is over, not held. This is the one ending on the screen where the member loses the deposit, so the bar says so.
CancelledInert tone, return-arrow badge, refund figure in the sub-line. Also stays hueless — a withdrawn fixture is finished, and the deposit came back.

FiltersSingle-select chips. Selected chip goes to the accent wash; the rest stay on #1C2129.

Empty listScreen 04: the Results header, then one bordered panel in place of the whole feed. No skeleton rows.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Hero banner — field gradient, triangle field, peak motif, prop-in-front layering
  • Card shell — the idea-card geometry, run full width instead of 268 px
  • Author row — portrait stacking, overlap and ring weight
  • Section container — the bordered #080A0C wrapper with its 1 px hairline
  • Status pill — the LIVE NOW treatment, reused for settlement dates
  • Icon button — 38 px circle, white 1.9-stroke glyph
  • Tab bar — built from the four active-state frames
  • Photography — all six portraits taken from the exports
  • Type scale, radii, surfaces — sampled, not approximated

New in this proposal

  • The verdict bar — no result component exists in the Figma at all; this is the whole proposal
  • Four tones, seven states, two colours — accent for passed, red for blocked, the 135° accent hatch for held, no hue for finished-and-quiet. The hatch is not new here: it is copied from veto.html, which settled it
  • Distinguishing badges — cross, ringed strike and ringed minus separate rejected, vetoed and ruled-out inside one red tone
  • Detail scale — one size step for the result page, so the same component reads at two sizes
  • One word per ending — the live build renders the same status as APPROVED on Results and PUBLISH IT! on Actions, and ships a never-rendered fourth string, Let's do it!
  • One green — #9BE222, against the four the live build uses for the same outcome
  • Publish it demoted from verdict to instruction — it now appears only in the Action card's next-step panel, where an imperative belongs; the verdict above it reads Approved
  • A red wash — rgba(226,86,74,.12) and ring rgba(226,86,74,.24), derived from the contract's semantic red at the same opacities as the accent wash. Not a new hue
  • An evidence line in every bar — the share, the threshold and the vote count. Live cards carry the outcome word and no figures at all, and no date
  • Taxonomy segmented control — Decisions / Positions, with its help button moved outside the track. The live control puts a label and a ? inside one 185 pt half with no separation, so a tap is a coin toss
  • Category chips, spelled out — On-field, Off-field, Position, Management, Decision. Deliberately colourless, so accent stays reserved for state; and full-length, fixing the live chip that truncates its own taxonomy to Manage…
  • Actions framing — the next-step panel, its owner line and its consequence line
  • Counting-round chart — elimination lines, threshold marker, animated outcome, on #1C2129 rather than the live pure #000000
  • Emphasis by weight and opacity, not hue — replacing the live six-colour legend (violet, blue, teal, brown, maroon, olive), three of whose dots sit within a shade of the card fill
  • Round tally — swatch weights matched to the chart so the two read as one figure. Final share replaces the heading Count events; Out · C4 replaces Eliminated C4; and the club-added option is labelled as such rather than styled identically to a member's
  • One tap target per card — a single full-width View details pill, against the three-to-four identical VIEW DETAILS strings a live viewport carries
  • Two radii, not seven — R16 for cards and containers, R12 for inner panels and verdict bars, plus the pill. The live Results list uses seven values in one viewport
  • An explainer that wraps — one sentence under the chart defining what a round is. The live explainer is clamped to a single line and breaks mid-word at countin…
  • Empty state — no empty result frame exists
  • No webfont link — the review host blocks external requests, so this file ships on the system stack only

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Are these the seven endings, and are they named right?

    The bar is built against the status values the live build emits, including rule_outed, which reads as a bug in a string table rather than a product state. We have shown it as Ruled out. If there are only four real endings this component gets simpler; if there are nine, we need the other two before build.

  2. Settled: a hold is the accent interrupted, not a hueless bar.

    This screen originally drew a veto hold as an inert bar — no hue, a pause badge — while the veto screens drew it as the accent struck through with static 135° bars. Both were defensible and they could not both ship. The hatch won, and this file now uses it: same tokens, same 5 px / 6 px rhythm, same rgba(155,226,34,.30) ring, same ⊘. Nothing else on the screen gets it — Lapsed and Cancelled stay hueless, because they are over. What we still need from you: confirm that a held proposal should keep appearing in the Results list at all, rather than staying in Voting until the appeal resolves. It currently sits in Results with a running clock, which is the one bar in the list that is not yet a result.

  3. What is the currency called, and is a forfeited deposit real?

    Three bars here talk about money. The live build uses four names for one thing — Credits, Participation Credits, Participation Deposit and a legacy BOND — and the string table still ships an unsubstituted [x] PARTICIPATION CREDITS. This screen now uses Credits as the noun and deposit in lower case for what happens to them, which is wallet.html's resolution rather than a fourth opinion. It also uses the wallet's figure: the deposit is 25 Credits, not the 250 this file first showed. The 25 is invented — no pricing exists anywhere in the repo — so it needs a product decision, and it is written up at length in the Create idea notes.

    The forfeiture conflict is settled. This screen used to show Ruled out taking the deposit while the wallet said a deposit always comes back. Neither was right. The live string table decides it: the deposit is refunded only when the Idea becomes a Proposal (ideaPage.agreement), it is forfeited when the Idea lapses (notification.idea_lapsed), and once it is a Proposal the member is charged nothing further — This will be free when the Proposal is Vetoed, Cancelled or Ruled Out. So there is exactly one way to lose the deposit: the idea lapses. Ruled out now reads deposit already returned, the Actions next-step line reads Deposit returned · 25 Credits, and Lapsed carries deposit forfeited. Wallet and Create idea now say the same thing. Confirm the rule and it is consistent everywhere.

  4. Two taxonomies overlap on one card. Which one survives?

    The segmented control splits the list into Decisions and Positions, while the chip on the card says On-field, Off-field or Management — and two cards in the list carry Position and Decision as chips instead. That is two classification systems in the same component. We need to know whether type and subject are separate axes, or whether one of them should go.

  5. Is the threshold always 70%?

    The chart draws a single 70% threshold, taken from the live detail screen's Threshold 70% line. But the club config carries four separate values — one each for positions, on-field decisions, off-field decisions and rejecting a veto. If the bar moves by proposal type then the dashed marker and the 70% needed figure in every verdict bar both have to read from config, and the chart needs to survive a threshold near 50%, where the marker would land on a gridline.

  6. Two status branches never fire in the current build — confirm what the bar will be handed.

    This is a build note rather than a design question, but it decides whether any of the seven states can be seen. func.dart::findStatusAction matches case "rule_outed " with a trailing space, while the enum emits rule_outed without one — so that branch is dead and every status that is not let_do_it or let_push_it falls through to the default and renders as Ruled out in the danger colour. findTypeAction carries the same trailing space on case "on_field_decisions ", so on-field items fall through to the wrong type label too. In practice an approved, lapsed or cancelled action is currently shown to members as ruled out, in red. We have built all seven states against the correct unspaced values; the two switch cases need the spaces removed before this component can show anything but one state.

    Two smaller ones while we are in there. Both en.json and vi.json ship has bene forfeited — for been — on the lapsed-idea message; the copy used here reads deposit forfeited. And the taxonomy tooltip still contains Lorem ipsum for both segments, so the help button beside the segmented control has no real copy to open with.

  7. Is the count really an elimination?

    The detail chart asserts five counting rounds with the lowest option dropped and its votes redistributed, and shows an option called Reject all options that the club added. That is the most defensible reading of a multi-option proposal, but it is an assumption about the vote engine, not something we found. If the count is a single plurality, the chart should be one bar per option and the tally loses its round column.
