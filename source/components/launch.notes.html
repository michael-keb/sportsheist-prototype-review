The five frames that run in front of the log-in screens: the splash a member sees on cold launch, what they see when that launch fails, the choice between logging in and signing up, what pressing Sign Up actually does in the shipped app today, and the screen that asks for notification permission before iOS does.

What you're looking at

The first four seconds of the app

Read frames 3 and 4 as a pair. Frame 3 is our proposal: Log In or Sign Up, both continuing inside the app. Frame 4 is the current shipped behaviour — the confirmation the live build raises the moment that sign-up control is pressed, telling you to go to the club's website, and then handing you to the phone's browser. There is no registration screen in the shipped app at all: one exists in the code but the only link to it is commented out. The Onboarding component's four-step sign-up, and the Sign Up button on frame 3, are proposals for a journey that today ends in a browser. Frame 4 is in the library so that cannot be misread.

Everything here happens before anyone has typed anything. The splash holds the screen while the stored sign-in token is checked; the entry fork sends a member to log in or a newcomer to sign up; the priming screen explains notifications before the phone's own permission alert appears. These frames hand straight over to the Log in component, so they take its geometry unchanged — the same 20px gutter, the same 64px button, the same Log in / Sign up wording.

In the current app three of these four screens either don't exist or don't work. The splash is the most brand-heavy screen in the product — a full-bleed violet-to-gold gradient with the logo centred — but it renders no loading indicator at all, so a slow sign-in check is indistinguishable from a frozen app. There is no failure screen: if the check fails the app silently drops the member into the signed-out flow, which looks exactly like being logged out. And the notification request fires on the first screen inside the app, with no explanation, at the same moment as a green success bar and an empty club page.

The rebuilt splash is made from this brand's own parts rather than a new picture: the near-black canvas, the two soft green glows already measured on Get Started, the faint angular triangles from the hero banners, the shipped logo, and the three acid peaks that the design system already uses as its loading mark. The peaks are deliberately doing two jobs at once — they are the brand shape and the thing that tells you the app is still working.

States

What varies

StateTreatment
Splash · checkingDefault. Lockup, animating peaks, Checking your session, and the 15-second promise at the foot. Recorded as having no indicator at all in the current app.
Splash · slowFrom about 4s. The promise line is replaced by a panel naming the wait: Still checking your session / your connection looks slow. Neutral fill, never red, and no button — the app is still working, so asking the member to act would be a lie. Undocumented in the current app.
Splash · timed outAt 15s the splash becomes frame 2. That is the whole point of the promise on frame 1.
Failure · offlineThe device has no connection. Neutral tile, last-synced time, Try Again plus Open What's Saved. Nothing red and nothing that suggests the member has been signed out.
Failure · serverThe device is online and we are not. Red tile, a reference number, Try Again plus Contact Us. The only state that admits we don't yet know the cause — it earns that by handing over a reference.
Failure · retryingPress Try Again. The accent fill drops to 55%, the label becomes Trying… with the accent ring, and the control stops accepting taps. The button keeps its exact width, so nothing on the frame moves.
Entry forkOne state. There is nothing to load and nothing to validate on this screen.
Leave-app confirm · shippedOne state, and that is the finding. NO closes the dialog; YES opens the phone's browser at <URL>/en/auth/signup and then closes the dialog. There is no loading state, no failure state, and no return path — if the browser cannot be opened the failure is printed to the debug console and the dialog closes anyway, so the member sees a dismissed dialog and nothing else. Whether the person then completes registration on the website is invisible to the app.
Priming · defaultOur screen, before the system alert. This is a screen the current app does not have.
Priming · declinedWhat a member sees after saying no to iOS — also missing from the current app, which records no follow-up either way. Neutral tile, Alerts are off, the honest explanation that we can't ask again, and a deep link to Settings. Not red, and not phrased as a mistake.
Reduced motionWith the phone's Reduce Motion setting on, the peaks and the retry ring stop dead rather than fading — the mark stays on screen at full contrast, it just doesn't move.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused, not redrawn

  • The logo — the shipped app_icon.svg and app_name.svg vector artwork, the same paths the Log in component uses. No monogram was approximated and the wordmark is not set as type.
  • Button, gutter and wording — 64px track, R32, 46px acid disc inset 9px, 20px gutter, Log in / Sign up. All measured off the Get Started Figma page by the Log in component; these frames simply obey it.
  • The glow discs — #276425 at 16%, from the Get Started frame.
  • The triangle field — the hero banner's 2.4–3% white triangles, scaled up.
  • The three peaks — the loading mark from the States kit, unchanged in geometry and timing.
  • Error panel and icon tile — the States kit's 52px tile, neutral and red variants, and its three-part copy structure.
  • Tick rows — the benefit row from Onboarding's pathway screen.
  • The dialog shell — the Dialogs kit's shell taken whole for frame 4: card, close disc, icon tile, title, body and the 46px two-button action row, at its own values. It is only re-scoped in the stylesheet, because this file already uses .btn for the 64px full-width action and the two definitions cannot both be global.
  • Frame 4's copy and behaviour — both strings, both button labels and the destination are read straight off the running code. Nothing on that frame is a design decision except the palette and the shell it is poured into.

New in this proposal

  • A loading indicator on the splash at all. The current screen has none, so a slow sign-in check shows a dead screen. This is the single most consequential change in the file.
  • A cold-start failure screen. Undefined today — splash.dart routes to the introduction flow after a one-second delay on both a real failure and a genuine sign-out, so a dropped connection is presented as being logged out. Both new states insist the member is still signed in.
  • A 15-second commitment, and the slow-connection panel that precedes it. A promise on screen that the wait will end in an answer.
  • The entry fork rewritten. The question is gone; one statement and two labelled destinations replace ARE YOU A MEMBER? / YES, CONTINUE / SIGN UP.
  • The guest note. The current copy says membership is required and the next screen offers a guest path. This says the true thing once, here.
  • The whole notification priming screen, plus the declined follow-up. Today the system alert fires unannounced on the first screen inside the app, alongside a success bar and an empty club page — three overlays in one frame.
  • Nothing on frame 4. It is the one frame in this file that proposes nothing — it records what ships. It is listed here only so it is clear it belongs in neither column: it is not reused from a Figma frame, and it is not something we are asking to build.
  • The greyscale iOS alert specimen, so it is clear exactly how much of that moment we control: the app's display name and the moment it fires. Nothing else.
  • Status caption in #99A0AE, not #5B606B. The States kit set the splash caption in the tertiary grey, which measures 2.56:1 and is reserved for decoration. The one line telling a member what the app is doing is not decoration, so it moves up to the 5:1 secondary grey.
  • Stacked lockup on the splash, horizontal everywhere else. A vertical lockup for the one screen with no other content; the horizontal lockup on the three that have some.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Is the entry fork a screen we still want?

    Get Started already has one button, and this screen exists only to ask which of two doors to open. Get Started could carry Sign Up as its main button with Already have an account? Log in underneath, and this screen would disappear — one less tap for every single person who opens the app. We have built the fork because Get Started is reproduced from the Figma file and we did not want to redraw it without asking, but our recommendation is to delete this screen and fold it into Get Started.

  2. Does signing up happen inside the app, or on the club's website?

    Frame 4 is the current answer, and frame 3 is the one we are proposing. The shipped app opens an external web page for sign-up — the LEAVING THE APP confirmation on frame 4, then the phone's browser at /en/auth/signup. The Onboarding component, meanwhile, builds a four-step registration flow inside the app, and a registration screen does exist in the code — but the only link to it is commented out, so nothing can reach it. These cannot both be true. We have assumed sign-up is in-app, because that is what Onboarding shows and because handing a brand-new user to a browser is where most of them are lost. If it stays on the web, frame 3's Sign Up button needs an outbound arrow, the copy needs to say where it goes, and Onboarding's four frames come out of the library.

  3. Frame 4's copy has three faults. Do we fix them, or is the whole screen going?

    Reproduced exactly as it ships, so they are visible rather than described. The word proeed is misspelled in the source. The buttons say NO and YES — answers to a question the dialog does not ask, and they come from the dialog presenter's defaults rather than from this screen, so no one chose them. And the dialog never names where YES goes. If sign-up moves in-app this screen disappears and none of it matters; if it stays, the copy needs rewriting and the buttons need to say Cancel and Open the website.

  4. Frame 4's dialog is English-only in every language. Intended?

    Both strings are hardcoded in the page's own source rather than looked up from the translation file, so they will not translate. The matching translation keys (exit_title, exit_content) exist and are correct — they are simply never used. It is a small code fix, not a design change, but it is worth catching before the app is offered in a second language.

  5. Is one clear statement the right replacement for the question?

    ARE YOU A MEMBER? asks about club membership but decides something else entirely — whether you already have a SportsHeist account — and its two answers, YES, CONTINUE and SIGN UP, answer neither reading of it. We replaced it with Everything runs off one account plus Log In and Sign Up, each labelled with where it goes. Confirm the wording; the structure — statement, two destinations, no question — we would hold to regardless.

  6. How long before the splash gives up, and is 15 seconds right?

    The frames promise an answer within 15 seconds, with the slow-connection panel appearing at 4 seconds. Both numbers are ours, chosen to be long enough to survive a bad train tunnel and short enough that nobody force-quits. They need to match whatever timeout the sign-in request actually uses, or the promise on screen becomes a lie.

  7. Is there anything saved to open when we're offline?

    The offline failure screen offers Open What's Saved and quotes a last-synced time, which assumes the app keeps a readable copy of the member's club. If it doesn't, that button and the last-synced line both come off and the screen has only Try Again — a materially worse experience, and a good argument for building the cache.

  8. Are these the four notifications we actually send?

    The priming screen lists a vote opening, 24 hours before it closes, the result, and replies to your own idea, and promises nothing else. That promise is only worth making if it is true, so the list needs checking against what the server really sends — and the “nothing else” line has to be honoured, not quietly broken by a later marketing push.

  9. What is the product called?

    Four spellings are in the shipped app: SPORTSHEIST in the logo, Sports Heist in body copy, and Sports Heist(stg) on the OS notification alert — a staging build suffix, with no space, shown to real users. We have used SportsHeist as one word throughout. The alert name comes from CFBundleDisplayName in the iOS build settings and the Android manifest label; it is a build fix rather than a design change, but it must be made before release.

  10. Two library-wide questions these frames inherit rather than settle

    Button height is 64px here, matching the Log in screens and the Figma button component. Several other kits standardised on 52px. And the verb pair is Log in / Sign up, where an earlier screen used Sign in / Sign up — two near-identical words on the same screen. Both need settling once, for the whole library; whatever is chosen, these frames and the Log in frames have to agree.
