The interruption kit: one modal shell in eleven specimens, plus toasts, loaders, the empty state and the inline banner. These are the components that tell a member something happened, ask them to confirm something, or make them wait. It now also carries an inventory of all 19 dialogs the app ships, and which of them should be deleted rather than redesigned.

What you're looking at

One shell replacing three, and a warning without amber

The app currently has three different modal shells across four screens — a hand-built dialog at about 78% width with no border and no shadow, a shared customSimpleDialog at a 16 pt inset with a raster image behind its title, and an untouched operating-system alert. It also has a full-bleed square-cornered snackbar sitting on top of the floating tab bar, and a loader that defaults to Material blue. This page defines one of each.

That is mostly a tidying job. The interesting problem is warning. Every other design system solves it with a third colour — amber — and the palette here has exactly two hues. So warning is built from the same red as error and separated from it by wash and ring alone: error is a red glyph on a red wash inside a red hairline ring; warning is the same red glyph on the neutral white wash with no ring. Same hue, one step quieter, and the words in the title carry the rest.

Everything on the page is a specimen laid out in the flow rather than overlaid, so every variant can be read at once. Two live examples open properly over the real scrim, four buttons fire real toasts into the dock, and one button demonstrates the in-button busy state.

Extended after the coverage audit. A count of the dialog layer found the kit was covering the wrong five. The app declares 19 dialog presenters and the two most-used of them were not in this kit at all: the generic server-error surface, referenced 27 times, which shows a member the words “INTERNAL SERVER”; and the general error, referenced 30 times, whose one control is a Vietnamese text link and which dismisses itself after five seconds. A third, the locker-room reminder, has ten references across two screens and had never been drawn. All three are now here, along with a shell for dialogs that carry form controls and a table listing every presenter, its reference count and whether it should be built or deleted. That table is the most useful thing on the page for scoping: five of the nineteen have no caller worth keeping.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

This is a system kit, and it is almost entirely proposed. No dialog, toast, loader, empty state or banner is designed in the new brand. What exists is the shipped behaviour and its copy — which screens interrupt, what they say, what they do next — and the shipped implementations are the thing being replaced rather than the thing being reproduced.

Reused from the Figma file

  • Tokens, surfaces and radii — sampled, not approximated
  • Section container — the bordered #080A0C wrapper, used for the spec panels and the empty state
  • Status pill — the LIVE NOW treatment, reused to mark the interactive panel
  • Tab bar — reconstructed from the active-state frames, so the toast clearance can be shown honestly
  • Icon construction — 24-box, round caps, optical stroke weight
  • Three-peak mark — the hero range silhouette, reused as the panel loader
  • Dialog content — the situations, addresses, counts and consequences come from the shipped flows
  • Reminder glyphs — the bell + plus and bell + check are lifted straight from all-locker-rooms.html's reminder trigger, so the dialog reads as the control that opened it
  • Reminder copy — the shipped KEEP / REMOVE pair and the three shipped states (set, cancel, set-confirmed) are preserved, in sentence case
  • Form control shell — forms.html's .ctl, unchanged but for the fill

New in this proposal

  • The dialog shell — one construction replacing the three the app ships
  • Five variants — success, error, warning, confirm, destructive, differing only in tile, title, body and action row
  • Warning without a third hue — the amber #E08A09 in the current colour file is dropped; warning is red on the neutral wash
  • Always-present dismiss — the shell it replaces offered no way out but the action, and one variant relied on a 5-second timeout
  • The verb convention — the affirmative repeats the title's verb, imperative, sentence case, three words at most; Got it is the only permitted generic, and only for an acknowledgement
  • Ink on red — #0B0E06, so both saturated fills behave the same way
  • Scrim — 72% plus a 10 px blur, against the flat ~45% no-blur backdrop that currently leaves the form behind the dialog fully legible
  • Focus trap and focus return
  • Toast — inset, card radius, semantic marker bar, icon, optional action, timed life. The shipped snackbar is the only full-bleed square-cornered element in the captured set: flat green, ~90 pt tall, no icon, no action, overlapping the floating tab bar
  • Loaders — the peaks and the two ring sizes, replacing a loader whose default paints a Material #2196F3 dot-ring, a hue that appears nowhere else in the brand
  • In-button busy state
  • Empty state — replacing a pure #000000 panel with a lilac border and one line of shouted display type, which offered nothing to do next
  • Inline banner — both variants; nothing like it exists
  • Eight drawn state icons — in place of two platform emoji
  • Server-error variant — the app's generic 5xx surface, referenced 27 times and never drawn. Cause, consequence, one next step and a quotable reference, replacing the words INTERNAL SERVER in 34 pt display type coloured #E04A09 over a raster
  • A reference code the member can quote — the 5xx surface currently gives support nothing to search on
  • Reminder dialog, all three states — ten references across Backchat and All Locker Rooms, never captured or drawn
  • A dialog that carries form controls — a shape the kit did not have; media.html should use this shell
  • One loader, not two — the second treatment and both Lottie assets are retired
  • No auto-dismissing dialogs — the 5-second timeout on the app's most-used error goes
  • Presenter inventory — all 19 presenters counted and ruled on, with five marked for deletion

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Can we delete five dialogs instead of rebranding them?

    The app declares 19 dialog presenters. Counting every non-comment reference in the code, five of them are not worth building, and the inventory table on the page marks each with a struck-through name and the word Delete. Four have no caller at all — showDialogNoButton, customActionSheet, showConfirmSP, and common, which has no external caller and is only reached through the two presenters we are replacing anyway. The fifth, showSuccess, has one caller and it should be a toast. Two more, showCustomDialogWithCustomContent and showNormalDialog, exist only to serve a single file and fold into the standard shell. customActionSheet is worth naming separately: it is the only Cupertino surface in the entire codebase, so deleting it removes the last iOS-styled component from an app that is otherwise Material throughout, at the cost of nothing, because nothing calls it. We need agreement that deleting is in scope — it takes roughly a quarter of the dialog layer off the build, and the alternative is paying to restyle code that never runs.

  2. Should the app's most-used error stop dismissing itself?

    showError is referenced 30 times, more than any other dialog. Its one control is a full-width text link reading Thoát — Vietnamese for “Exit” — and it calls .timeout(Duration(seconds: 5)), which pops the dialog whether or not the member has finished reading. Six of its callers are the API layer, so the body is a relayed server string whose length nobody controls. We have assumed no dialog auto-dismisses, the label becomes a real button in English, and the null-title fallback becomes Something went wrong rather than the current Cảnh báo (“Warning”), which mislabels every error. If any of the 30 sites genuinely wants a message that disappears, that site wants a toast, and we should list them.

  3. How much should a member be told about a 5xx?

    showInternal is referenced 27 times and shows a member the words INTERNAL SERVER. We have replaced it with cause, consequence, one next step and a support reference — SH-500 · 4F2A9C · 18:24 CET. Two things need confirming: that the reference format is something your support process can actually search on, and that Get help has somewhere to go. If there is no support channel behind that button, the dialog should be single-action and the reference should still be copyable.

  4. Does setting a reminder need a dialog at all?

    The shipped flow puts three modals in a row — ask, loading, confirm — so a member taps a bell and dismisses two panels to end up where the bell already showed them they were. Meanwhile all-locker-rooms.html toggles the bell optimistically with no dialog. We recommend toggle for set, dialog only for remove, toast for both outcomes, and have built all three states so you can see what is being given up. Whichever way this goes, the list and the dialog kit have to agree, because the bell is the only entry point.

  5. Is warning distinguishable enough from error?

    This is the one genuinely uncomfortable decision in the kit. With two hues available, warning and error share a colour and differ only by wash and ring — red glyph on a red wash inside a red ring for error, red glyph on the neutral wash with no ring for warning. It reads correctly side by side, and the title always says which it is, but they are two steps of one signal rather than two signals. The alternative is admitting a third hue for warning alone, which the contract forbids and which we would rather not open. We need you to look at the two specimens together and tell us it holds.

  6. What should each dialog's affirmative actually say?

    The app currently uses five different affirmatives across four dialogs — three of them shouted in caps, and two of them still in Vietnamese from the source project. We have replaced them with a convention: repeat the title's verb, imperative, sentence case. Someone who owns the product language needs to sign off the resulting labels, because the convention is only as good as the titles it derives from.

  7. Toast, or dialog?

    Several flows currently interrupt with a modal where a toast would do — a saved profile does not need a dismissal. We have assumed dialogs are for decisions and failures a member must act on; toasts are for confirmations and recoverable errors. Applying that rule will change which control several existing screens use, so it should be agreed before build rather than screen by screen.

  8. How long should a toast live?

    4 seconds, or 6 when it carries an action, are our numbers — long enough to read, short enough not to block the tab bar. If a toast can be the only notification of a failed vote, it is the wrong component for that message and it should be a banner that persists.

  9. Does anything besides delete get a red button?

    We have restricted the filled red to irreversible destruction of something the member owns. Leaving a locker room, withdrawing an idea and cancelling a vote are all arguably destructive and all currently use it. Drawing that line narrowly is what keeps the red meaningful.
