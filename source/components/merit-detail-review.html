What you're looking at

Four screens behind the Merits tab

The Merits tab shows a member where they stand. These four screens answer the questions that follow: how did I get here, what else is there to win, how do I win this one, and — the only one the member doesn't ask for — you just won something.

The current app has an ACHIEVEMENTS screen with two tabs, both captured empty, and a separate HALL OF FAME list. Between them they use four different golds (#D4AF37, #B28401, #DFAA11 and a declared weaponBronzeColor), and none of the four is a brand colour. #DFAA11 is literally colorVeto — the amber the app uses to show a proposal being blocked. So today a celebration and a veto are painted the same colour.

The new palette has no gold and no metallics at all, so the badges are not medals. They are drawn from the brand's own geometry: a peak, held in a cell. The glyph inside says what the member did; the cell around it says how rare the badge is. The whole system is one accent #9BE222, the surface ladder and the shape of the cell — no second hue anywhere on the page. This is the biggest decision on these screens and the one worth your attention first: see Badge artwork in the anatomy table and Rarity in the states table.

The four are separate screens, and each one has its own phone above, numbered and captioned. They are pushed routes, so they carry a back arrow rather than the tab bar.

Anatomy

Four screens, one device each: 1 merit history, 2 badge collection, 3 badge detail and 4 award moment. The Region column says which screen a row belongs to. The first three carry their own hero and back control; the award moment is presented over the collection and carries its own dismiss.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused from the Figma file

  • Token block — copied verbatim from merits.html, itself sampled from the War Room and Backchat dark-mode exports. Not one value invented.
  • Hero banner — gradient, field triangles and the three accent peaks, unchanged.
  • Three-peak range motif — the same path data as the hero, reused at 196 × 52 as the centrepiece of the award moment.
  • Section container, status pill, icon button, pill button, row, avatar — all from merits.html.
  • Summary card — the standings card construction, re-pointed at a season total.
  • Tab-bar glyphs — the War Room, Backchat and Merits icons, reused at 17px to attribute each ledger entry to its source surface.
  • Portraits — six faces from _assets/avatars.css.
  • Blurred backdrop — the one thing worth keeping from the live congratulations modal.

New in this proposal

  • The badge system — cell plus glyph, drawn from the peak motif. Replaces four non-brand golds, a stock laurel pair, two unrelated crown illustrations and a concave-cornered plaque frame with one geometry that costs nothing to extend.
  • Rarity by facet count — circle → octagon → hexagon → triangle. A distinction carried by shape and ring opacity instead of a new hue, per the palette rule.
  • Progress on the perimeter — the accent arc riding the cell's own facets. The live build has no progress indicator anywhere in this area.
  • Locked and in-progress states — neither exists today; no locked silhouette, tier marker or progress indicator appears on any of the six captured screens.
  • Merit ledger — grouped by period with a subtotal per group. The nearest thing in the code is ItemCreditTransactionHistory, a bare title-and-date pair.
  • Dates that resolve — no date in the live capture set carries a year, on a surface filtered by season. Here the season sits in the status pill and the period in the group header, so 28 Dec is never ambiguous.
  • The award moment — angular ray fan on a radial fade, the peaks rising, the badge cresting them, a Share action and a ✕ dismiss. The live version is a static card with generic three-ball clip-art in #DFAA11 — the veto amber — with no motion, no share and no way out other than the CTA.
  • Hero eyebrow on pushed routes — a tracked micro-label above the title so the route name can be short enough not to collide with the peaks.
  • Reversal row — the semantic red used once, for merits taken back.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Does the badge collection replace Achievements and Hall of Fame?

    Today there are three overlapping status surfaces: Achievements (tabbed Playoff titles / Leader status, both captured empty), Hall of Fame (a podium plus a holders list), and Credit/Reputation. We have assumed one badge collection replaces the Achievements tabs, and that Hall of Fame survives as the Who holds it section on each badge plus the podium already built on the Merits tab. If Hall of Fame must stay a separate destination, say so — it changes the navigation, not the artwork.

  2. Is the badge geometry the right answer to the gold problem?

    This is the decision to push back on if you are going to. The live badges use #D4AF37, #B28401, #DFAA11 and a bronze token; the new palette has no gold and no metallics, and #DFAA11 is the veto colour. Rather than invent a metal, we made rarity structural — facet count and ring opacity — so the whole system runs on one accent. The trade is that rarity is quieter than gold: it is legible, but it does not shout across a room. If you want the loudest possible Elite badge we can go further on scale and stroke weight, but we will not add a hue.

  3. What is a merit worth, and can it be taken away?

    The ledger figures are placeholders: Member of the Match +250, badge earned +100, veto held +80, seven-day streak +70, motion carried +60, votes cast +40, replies upvoted +36, member invited +50. We have also built one −40 reversal for a vote withdrawn before the motion closed, in the semantic red. If merits only ever go up, that row comes out and the red never appears on this surface. The live app also calls this currency three things — points, Reputation Points and credit transactions; we have used merits throughout and recommend retiring the other two.

  4. Are these the right twelve badges?

    Only one badge name in the entire code snapshot is real: Member of the Match/Week/Season. The other eleven — First Vote, Backchat Regular, Derby Day, Recruiter, Streak Keeper, Top Ten, Veto Held, Kingmaker, Full Season, Founding Member, Hall of Fame — are our proposal, chosen so that every one of the app's four surfaces has something to earn. Confirm the roster and the earning rules; the artwork system absorbs any number of badges without change.

  5. What are the rarity bands?

    We have assumed Common = most members, Uncommon = 10–40%, Rare = 1–10%, Elite = under 1%, computed live from the holder count rather than assigned by hand. If rarity is instead editorial — someone decides a badge is Elite — the bands need naming and the legend copy changes.

  6. Where does Share go?

    We have assumed Backchat, since it is the app's own social surface and keeps the member inside the product. The alternative is the OS share sheet to Instagram and X, which needs a designed share card at story dimensions — a separate piece of work.

  7. The default avatar has to be replaced.

    Not a question so much as a blocker. The current default avatar is watermarked marketing key-art: the circular crop cuts the burned-in wordmark to SPORTSHEI, and a red …WED BY 1.00 PIXELS watermark plus a yellow-and-black hazard bar are plainly legible at the 90pt podium size. The same image renders five times on one Hall of Fame screen. Every portrait on these frames comes from _assets/avatars.css instead, but a licensed or generated default-avatar asset is still needed before any of this ships. We would propose a badge cell with no glyph as the fallback — it is already in the file and needs no new art.

  8. One thing to confirm rather than decide.

    These are pushed routes, so they carry a back arrow and no tab bar — matching how the live Achievements and Hall of Fame routes behave. If you would rather the tab bar stayed visible throughout, all four frames gain 58px of bottom chrome and the moment's card moves up.
