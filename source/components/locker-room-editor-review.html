Schedule a
locker room

The create-and-edit editor behind Backchat's + control, in the dark rebrand. Twelve phone-width frames: compose, schedule, the three pickers, invites, review, created, edit, delete and validation. The invite sheet at the foot is live.

What the screen actually collects

  • topicString · the room's one line of text
  • start_atISO 8601 UTC · one instant, no end
  • is_open_roombool · open, or locked
  • invite_membersuuid[] · invitees only

Four fields, and that is the whole payload. BackChatRoomEditorRequestDTO carries no description, no duration, no end time, no speaker list and no cover art, so none is drawn here. All four are raised as decisions in the notes instead.

Offsets run from the clock, not from the date already in the field, and round up to the next quarter hour. Now is Thursday 30 July, 4:19 PM.

The offsets are the six shipped values — now(0) · inAnHour(1) · inTwoHours(2) · inThreeHours(3) · inFiveHours(5) · inTwelveHours(12) hours. There is no four-hour step in the enum. Two behaviours are corrected. In the shipped sheet each offset adds its hours to editorSelectedDateTime — the value already in the field — so pressing In twelve hours on a room already set for next Sunday quietly moves it to Monday morning, and the sheet resets its highlight to Now every time it opens no matter what is stored. Here the offsets run from the live clock, they roll the date when they cross midnight, and the readout says so. And Now cannot ship as it stands: the field is seeded with the moment the screen opened, so +0 resolves to a time that is already in the past when _validated() compares it to a fresh DateTime.now() — it always fails. Minutes are quarters here, following sheets.html; the shipped picker uses minutesInterval: 1, so a room can be scheduled for 8:37 PM.

The start-time rule, as built

  • FloorAt least 5 minutes from now. Enforced at the picker AND at submit, so nothing can be typed past it. The shipped build enforces only isBefore(DateTime.now()), and only on the Create tap.
  • CeilingOne year out. The shipped calendar bounds are 2010-10-16 to 2030-03-14, which permit a room in 2011.
  • DaysToday onwards. Past days are struck out and inert instead of silently ignoring the tap.
  • MinutesQuarter hours, following sheets.html. The shipped picker allows any minute, so 8:37 PM is a legal start.
  • OffsetsNow · In an hour · In two hours · In three hours · In five hours · In twelve hours — the six shipped LockerRoomEditorTimePickerPadding values, at +0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 12 hours. Computed from the clock, and they roll the date.

What you're looking at

The biggest screen in Backchat, and the only one with no design

This editor is the largest single file in the Backchat feature — 1,207 lines — and there is no Figma frame for it, in either the old violet theme or the new dark one. It has only ever existed as code. So this is the first time it has been designed, rather than translated, and the twelve frames on the left are laid out in the order a host actually moves through: write the topic, choose who can get in, set when it opens, invite people, review, and publish. Then the same screen again as Edit room, with the delete path.

The thing that shaped the whole design is how little the room actually stores. The request the app sends carries exactly four pieces of information: the topic, the moment it opens, whether it is open or locked, and who is invited. There is no description, no duration, no end time and no speaker list anywhere in it. Rather than draw fields that no part of the system could accept, the screen collects those four things properly and states the consequences of the gaps — for example, Duration appears as a read-only line saying Open-ended, because a room genuinely has no scheduled end. Whether it should is one of the questions at the foot of this page.

Three things in the shipped build are broken rather than merely undesigned, and all three are fixed here. Two shipped strings are wrong and are corrected on screen — the delete dialog reads Delete this rooms? and the validation dialog reads Start at must greater than current time. And the Now shortcut on the time picker can never succeed: it resolves to the moment the screen was opened, which the app then rejects for being in the past. Details in States.

Provenance

What is reused and what is new

Reused, unchanged

  • Every form control — forms.html: the label convention, the 52px shell, the focus ring, the error ring, the inline validation row and the subgrid two-up primitive.
  • All three pickers — sheets.html: the sheet shell and grabber, the calendar, the three-column time lists, the offset pills, the option row and the sticky in-sheet search.
  • All three dialogs — dialogs.html: the shell, the 44px icon tile, the 46px action pair and the scrim.
  • The created-room card — backchat.html's .room, so the preview is literally the component the club will see.
  • Frame chrome — create-idea.html's numbered phone-width frames.
  • The six time offsets — the shipped LockerRoomEditorTimePickerPadding values and their exact labels.
  • The room-type metaphor — the padlock pair from the shipped tiles, and the open room / locked room wording.
  • Room card in the success dialog — the shipped dialog already does this, and it was the right instinct.
  • Portraits and usernames — _assets/avatars.css and the same members the rest of the library uses.

New in this proposal

  • A review step before publishing. Today the Create tap fires the POST and the invites go out; the room is only reviewed after it exists. Frame 08 reads all four fields back and names the invitees.
  • A docked CTA. The shipped button sits at the bottom of the scroller below the invite list, so a room with ten invitees pushes it off screen.
  • An 80-character topic limit and its counter. No limit exists in the code, and the room card clamps to two lines.
  • A stated start-time rule — at least 5 minutes out, at most a year, quarter hours only. The shipped bounds are 2010-10-16 to 2030-03-14 with minutesInterval: 1, so today a room can legally be scheduled for 8:37 PM in 2011.
  • Offsets computed from the clock, rolling the date when they cross midnight, instead of adding hours to whatever is already in the field.
  • Named-day date format — Sun 2 Aug and a full summary line, replacing MM/dd/yyyy with no order hint.
  • A host row in the invite list and the sentence explaining that speakers are promoted inside the room, not here.
  • Second-line context on search results (In 4 of your rooms) and the already-invited pinned to the top of the sheet.
  • Inline validation in place of the two OOPS modals, plus a 2 to fix counter in the app bar.
  • The access-change warning on edit.
  • A working invite-later checkbox that gates review.
  • Grouped, disabled and struck-out calendar days, and a disabled month-back control.

Nothing here uses a colour outside the sampled tokens plus the one semantic red #E2564A. The shipped screen's palette does not survive: bgLabel #F9F9F9, borderButton, primaryOpacityColor and the colorFrozenBGVotingPregress constant borrowed from the Voting feature are all light-theme values, and the four-stop gradient strip built from that last one — the fade over the offset row — is gone, replaced by the row simply scrolling.

Decisions

What we need from you

  1. Should a room have a description?

    The request carries a topic and nothing else, so the topic line is the entire pitch for the room. On a phone that is one or two lines of text to persuade someone to give up an evening. We have built it with topic only and added an 80-character cap so it cannot overflow the card. If a description is wanted it needs a new field on the endpoint, a decision about whether it appears on the card or only in the room, and a length.

  2. Should a room have a duration or an end time?

    Nothing in the request or the room record carries one, so a room runs until the host closes it — and if the host never closes it, presumably forever. We have stated this on screen as Open-ended rather than hide it. Two things argue for adding a duration: attendees plan around it, and an abandoned room currently has no defined ending. A scheduled end also changes the room card, which today shows only a start.

  3. What is the minimum notice for a new room, and is there an "open it now" path?

    This is the one that blocks build. The Now offset is currently unusable — it always fails validation — so a host who wants a room immediately has no working route. We have assumed a 5-minute floor and made Now mean the next quarter hour, which is 11 minutes away in the frames. The alternatives are a shorter floor, or splitting start a room now into its own action that opens the room immediately rather than scheduling it. Which of the three do you want?

  4. Can a host name speakers before the room opens?

    Today they cannot. The request sends one flat list of invitees; the room comes back with a hosts[] array carrying permissions[] and mic_on per person, which means roles exist but are assigned at runtime, inside the room. We have built invitees only, and said so on screen. If a host should be able to line up two co-speakers when scheduling, that is a new field on the request and a new role state on the invite row — the row is already built to take one.

  5. Should the club see who else is invited before they join?

    The room card shows a count and the host, not the guest list. For an open room the count is the draw; for a locked room the list might be the whole reason to attend. We have kept the guest list private to the editor, matching the card as built, but it is a product call rather than a technical one.

  6. Confirm the app bar wording: "Schedule a room" or "Start a room"?

    The shipped bar says START A ROOM, which reads as "open one now" on a screen whose whole purpose is to pick a future time. We have used Schedule a room, with Edit room in the edit mode. If question 3 gains a separate immediate path, Start a room becomes the right name for that and this one stays Schedule.

  7. Two library-wide items this screen inherits.

    Control height is still open between 52px and 64px; this file uses 52px because every control on it comes from forms.html. And a failed delete currently closes the screen as though it had worked — deleteLockerRoom catches its own exception and only prints in debug — so the kit's error toast needs wiring to it at build.
